Once one computer hosts your vault, or you run your own server, every other device you use pairs with
it and works on the same notes. Pairing is a one-time step per device; afterwards the device connects
on its own.

## Pair with a desktop host

A desktop host takes devices only over the network you choose, and only devices you approve on the
hosting computer. Each device makes its own key when it pairs and is known by its certificate from
then on; there is no password or token to copy around.

### Before you start

- The vault is hosted on your Mac or Linux computer ([Ways to run AIDE](/docs/getting-started/ways-to-run/#host-a-local-vault)).
- The host's key is kept in the system's secure store: the keychain on the Mac, an unlocked keyring
  (GNOME Keyring, KeePassXC…) on Linux. Without one, **Devices over the network** says "This needs the
  host's key in the keychain" and stays off, because a key kept in a file could be copied along with
  the host's data. On Linux, start a keyring and open AIDE again.
- The other device can reach the hosting computer. With [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/) on both,
  that works from anywhere; on the same Wi-Fi or LAN, a local address works at home.

### Let devices in

<Steps>

1. On the hosting computer, open the host's settings: **Settings › Host** on the Mac, **Settings ›
   General** (the **Host** group) on Linux. Choose **Devices over the network…**.
2. Under **Where devices reach this Mac** (on Linux, **Where devices reach this computer**), pick an
   **Address**. The list has this computer's Tailscale addresses first, then its LAN addresses; the
   host listens on port 8443 by default.
3. Choose **Let devices in**. The host restarts, and **Now** shows **Taking devices** with each
   address's state and the host key's fingerprint.

</Steps>

**Turn off** stops taking devices over the network. If the computer has no Tailscale or LAN address,
the page says so and there's nothing to pick.

### Add a device

<Steps>

1. On the hosting computer, in **Devices over the network**, choose **Add a device…**. AIDE shows a
   **Link** that starts with `aide://pair-host?`. It works for 5 minutes, for one device.
2. On the other device, open AIDE's **Get started** screen and paste the link into **Pairing link**
   under **Connect to a host**, then choose **Connect to the host**. On an iPhone you can open the link
   instead, and it fills the field.
3. The device shows its name and its key's fingerprint, and the host lists it under **Asking to
   pair** with a fingerprint too. Check that they match.
4. Choose **Approve** on the host ("Approved: it's connecting"), or **Deny**.

</Steps>

Every connection after that checks the host's key, so a device never talks to another machine posing
as your host. A device's certificate lasts two years and renews itself when it connects in its last
60 days; one that has expired has to pair again.

<Aside>
The link is shown as text, not a QR code. Send it to the other device the way you like (AirDrop, a
note in another app, a message to yourself); it is useless after 5 minutes or once a device has
used it.
</Aside>

### What a paired device can do

A device connected to a host works with the notes, todos, daily notes, search, the Feed, plugin tabs,
AI and Organize, and receives its own notifications. Everything that changes the host itself stays on
the hosting computer: pairing, the device list, AI keys, plugin settings, Feed subscriptions and MCP.

To disconnect a device, choose **Sign out** in its **Settings › Devices**. That forgets the host on
this device; the device's certificate stays valid until it is removed on the host.

## Pair with your own server

A server pairs devices with a single-use code. Pairing needs the server's address, and anything but
a server on your own computer must be HTTPS.

<Steps>

1. Get a pairing code:
    - on the server, run `aide pair`, or
    - on a device that's already connected, open **Settings › Devices** and choose
      **Pair another device**.
2. On the new device, in **Get started › Your AIDE server** (on Linux, **Connect to a server**), enter
   the **Server** address, such as `https://aide.my-tailnet.ts.net`, and the **Pairing code**.
3. Choose **Connect**.

</Steps>

A code looks like `XXXXX-XXXXX`, works once and expires after 10 minutes. At most five unused codes
exist at a time. After 10 wrong codes from one address within 10 minutes, the server refuses further
attempts from it for a while.

When the server knows its public address (`AIDE_PUBLIC_URL`), `aide pair` also prints a link,
`aide://pair?server=…&code=…`. Opening it on a device fills in both fields.

[Run your own server](/docs/self-hosting/own-server/) covers putting HTTPS in front of the server
with Tailscale, Cloudflare Tunnel or Caddy.

## Manage devices

### On your own server

On a device connected to a server, **Settings › Devices** lists every paired device with when it was
last seen; this one is marked **This device**.

- **Revoke** next to another device cuts it off. Its next request fails and it signs out.
- **Sign out** revokes this device first, then forgets the server. Drafts stay on the device for when
  you reconnect. If the server can't be reached, **Sign out without revoking** appears: the device
  forgets the server but stays authorized until you revoke it from another device.

### On a desktop host

Devices are managed on the hosting computer, never from a paired device: a paired device's
**Settings › Devices** shows the list, but revoking happens on the host.

- **Linux host:** **Settings › Devices** lists the devices paired with the host, with **Revoke** for
  each. A revoked device is cut off at once, its open connections closed.
- **Mac host:** this release has no device list on the hosting Mac. To shut every paired device out,
  choose **Turn off** in **Devices over the network**; the host then takes no device over the
  network.

Restoring a host from a backup revokes every device; they pair again with the restored host.

### A lost device

- **With your own server:** revoke it in **Settings › Devices** on any other connected device. If you
  have lost every device, run `aide pair` on the server and pair a new device with that code. Access
  to the server machine is the proof that you own it.
- **With a desktop host:** revoke it on the hosting computer as described above.

## Notifications on your phone

The server sends a notification for every Feed event triaged **Act on**, and for **Need to know**
events unless you turn that off: **Settings › Notifications › Also push "need to know"** (on Linux,
**Also notify "need to know"**). Everything else waits in the Feed. Plugins can also mark a single
event to always or never notify.

- **Mac and Linux** fetch their notifications from the server or host themselves; nothing else is
  needed.
- **iPhone** notifications go through Apple's push service, which the server reaches through a push
  relay (or its own APNs key when you build the app yourself). There is no public push relay yet, so an
  iPhone gets no push notifications today; the events are in its Feed when you open it.

See [The Feed](/docs/guides/feed/) for how events are triaged.
