AIDE has three ways to look across your notes: search finds one, the graph shows how they connect, and Review shows what you got done in a week, month, quarter or year.

## Searching notes

On the Mac and Linux, **Open Quickly…** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>O</kbd> or <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>F</kbd>; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>O</kbd> or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> on Linux) searches every note:

1. First, notes whose title or path matches what you type.
2. Then, under **In text**, notes whose text matches, each with a snippet of where.

Arrow keys move through the list and <kbd>Return</kbd> opens a note; a text match opens with the find bar on the words it found. <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Enter</kbd> on Linux) opens it in a new tab.

When no note has the title you typed, the last row is **Create “…”**: choose it, or press <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd> (<kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Enter</kbd> on Linux) whatever row is chosen, and AIDE makes a note with that title and opens it. It's a file of that name at the top of your vault, or in the folders you typed (`projects/Launch plan`). The command palette's **Open a random note** opens any note that isn't a scratch note.

The search field at the top of the Wiki does the same on every device, the iPhone included: title and path matches under **Notes**, text matches under **In text**.

Inside one note, **Find in Note…** (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>F</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>F</kbd>, or **Find** in the note's bar on the iPhone) opens the editor's find bar.

## Backlinks

**Backlinks** lists the notes that point at the note you're reading. On the Mac and Linux it's a view of the right panel (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd> on Linux), next to the outline; on the iPhone, open it from the note's **More › Backlinks**.

- **Linked mentions**: each line of another note with a link that opens this note, the same way a click resolves it: a `[[wikilink]]`, an embed or a relative Markdown link.
- **Unlinked mentions**, folded: lines of notes that name this note by its title or an alias without linking it. Code, links and web addresses don't count. **Link** beside a line turns that name into a link to this note, in place: `[[Name]]` as you wrote it when that opens the note, otherwise a link by its path that still reads as you wrote it. Only that line changes, and only if it still reads as shown.

Click a line to open its note at that line.

## Pinned

Pin the notes you come back to and they're listed under **Pinned** at the top of the Wiki, above the folders. Choose **Pin** in a Wiki row's menu, or **Pin this note** in the command palette while the note is open; **Unpin** takes it off. Pins belong to the device, so each device keeps its own.

## The graph

The graph draws your wiki and periodic notes as dots, sized by how many links they have, with a line for each link between them. Links are resolved the same way as clicking them in a note.

- On the Mac and Linux it's the **Graph** section of the sidebar: <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>4</kbd> on the Mac, <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>4</kbd> on Linux.
- On the iPhone, open it from the Wiki's **More › Graph**.

Drag the background to move around and drag a dot to move it. Pinch, or scroll with <kbd>⌘</kbd> held on the Mac, to zoom. Click a dot to open its note; <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click opens it in a new tab.

The bar across the top has:

- **Search** ("Find notes, paths, #tags"): marks the notes whose title, path or tags contain every word you type. <kbd>Return</kbd> opens the note titled exactly that, else the first match.
- **Tags**: adds each tag as a dot of its own, linked to the notes that carry it.
- **This note**: shown while a note is open; narrows the graph to that note and the notes up to two links away.
- The number of notes and links shown.

### Watching the assistant work

The graph can play what the assistant did in a turn, step by step: the
notes it was given (grey), what its search found (purple), what it read (teal), what its proposals
would change (orange) and what its answer drew on (green). Each note lights up as its step comes and
keeps the numbers of the steps that touched it; a pulse runs from your question to it, and the notes
linked to it flicker for a moment without lighting up. Everything else dims.

- On the Mac and Linux, keep the assistant open in the right panel and ask while the **Graph** section
  shows: the turn plays as it runs.
- On the iPhone, tap **Ask** on the graph: the assistant opens at half height and the turn plays above
  it.
- Any reply that touched notes has **Show on graph**, which opens the graph and plays that turn
  again.

The bar at the bottom lists the steps (click one to go there), shows the current one, and has play,
pause and a scrubber; × clears it. With Reduce Motion on, the notes light up and keep their numbers
without the flare, pulse or flicker. Notes the graph doesn't show (with **This note**, or filtered by a
search) are counted as not in the graph.

## Review

Review lists the todos you completed in a period: the record to look back on at the end of a week, or to copy into a performance review. Open it with **Review** in the Wiki's header (on the iPhone, **More › Review**), or from the command palette.

- Choose **Week**, **Month**, **Quarter** or **Year**, and move to the previous or next one with the arrows. Weeks start on the day set in **Settings › Wiki**.
- Three counts sit at the top: todos **done**, the **notes** they were in, and **active days**.
- When that period's note exists, its `## Summary` section shows as the period's summary. While the period is turned on in **Settings › Wiki**, a button opens its note (creating it if needed).
- **By day** groups the todos by the day they were completed; **By note** groups them by the note they're in, the note with the most first.
- A week also looks ahead: **Still open** lists every [old todo](/docs/guides/todos/#old-todos) that's still open, the oldest first, with how many days old it is and "old since this week" on the ones that just turned old. **Tidy up…** opens Todos' Tidy up to decide on them.

A todo counts on the day of its completion date (` ✅ 2026-10-22`). One without a date counts for the day of the daily note it's in, marked "recorded". Only done todos (`[x]`) count; cancelled and migrated ones don't. See [Todos](/docs/guides/todos/) for how completion dates are written.

**Share** (Mac and iPhone) or **Copy as Markdown** (Linux) gives the period as Markdown, grouped the way the screen is:

```markdown
## 2026-W43 — 12 done

### 2026-10-20
- [x] Ship the release notes — Release v0.5
- [x] Call the bank — 2026-10-20
```
