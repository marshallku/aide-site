The assistant plans your day with you. You tell it what you want ("Plan my day", "I lost the
afternoon, what can move?"), and it answers with a short reply and concrete changes to your todos:
a time block, an estimate, a new todo, a move to another day. Nothing changes until you take a
change, unless you tell it to apply them right away.

The assistant runs in the app on your device. It needs a model: see
[Where the model comes from](#where-the-model-comes-from).

## Open it

- **Mac and Linux:** the **Assistant** button in the Todos header opens it in the right panel,
  beside whatever you're looking at.
- **iPhone:** the **Assistant** button under Todos' **More** menu opens it in a sheet over Today.
  You can keep using Todos below it.

Type a message, or start with one of the suggestions: **Plan my day**, **Re-plan the rest of
today**, **What can move to tomorrow?** On the Mac, <kbd>Return</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd>
sends; on Linux, <kbd>Enter</kbd> sends and <kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new line. The
button beside Send starts a **New conversation**.

<Badge text="Next release" /> On the Mac, <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd> starts a new line too (on the
iPhone, Return starts a new line and Send sends). You don't have to wait for an answer: a message
you send while the assistant is still answering waits under it as **Sends after this answer**, and
goes once the answer is in, with the answer in what it knows. Several wait in the order you sent
them, and ✕ takes one back before it goes. If the answer asks to read a note, what waits holds until you choose **Allow** or **Deny**. A turn that fails says why with **Retry**, which sends the same message again.

<Badge text="Next release" /> To add a todo without asking, start the message with `/todo`: each line after it goes to today's todos at once, even while an answer is coming, and the conversation says what was added ([A todo instead of a note](/docs/guides/scratch/#a-todo-instead-of-a-note)). The command list shows as soon as you type `/`.

## What it sees

Each message goes to the model with a picture of your day, built on your device:

- your open todos, grouped as today (in today's daily note, or due or scheduled today), overdue,
  upcoming (the next 7 days) and later, up to 120 of them, with their time blocks, estimates,
  priorities and dates;
- today's calendar events, the time now, your workday (default 09:00–18:00) and the free time in it
  (gaps under 10 minutes left out), and how many minutes today's todos still ask for;
- your memory and goals notes (below), and what you finished this week;
- the last 12 messages of the conversation.

When the calendar can't be read, the assistant is told the day isn't known to be free, and you see
a notice.

## Proposals: Take, Drop, Undo

The reply comes with proposal cards, one per todo it wants to change and one per new or moved todo.
A card shows a summary such as `10:00–11:00 · Write the report`, why, and warnings when they apply:
**Already past**, **Outside your workday**, **Overlaps** (an event, another block, or another
proposal).

The assistant can propose to:

| Change | What happens to the note |
|---|---|
| Schedule / unschedule | Gives the todo a time block (`10:30 - 12:00 …`) or takes it away. |
| Estimate | Sets how long it should take, 5 minutes to 12 hours (`[estimate:: 45m]`). |
| Status | Marks it open, in progress, done or cancelled. |
| Add | A new todo in that day's daily note, today or up to a year ahead. |
| Move | The todo, with its memo and subtasks, goes to another day's daily note; it is marked migrated `[>]` where it was. |
| Someday <Badge text="Next release" /> | Adds your someday tag, so the todo waits under **Someday / maybe** ([old todos](/docs/guides/todos/#old-todos)). |

- **Take** (✓) applies one card. **Drop** (✕) discards it. **Take all** takes every waiting card of
  a reply, in order.
- Open proposals also show on **Todos**: a change under the todo it changes, a new todo in a
  **Proposed** section at the top, each with ✓ and ✕.
- A taken card has **Undo**. It puts the line back only if it's still exactly as the assistant left
  it; if you've edited it since, Undo says so and leaves your note alone.
- A card can be taken for 24 hours. When you take it later, its times are checked again against the
  day as it is now; a new clash asks you to choose **Take anyway**.
- Taking a card twice, here or on another device, applies it once.

Every applied change leaves a small action record at the end of the note it changed (shown as a
marker in the editor). That record is how AIDE knows a change was applied and how Undo finds it.

### Ask for each change, or apply right away

**Settings › Assistant › Changes** (on Linux, the **Apply right away (yolo)** switch):

- **Ask for each change** (the default): proposals wait until you take or drop each one.
- **Apply right away (yolo)**: every proposal that passes the checks (a real todo, a valid time) is
  applied at once. Each can still be undone from its card.

Proposals made when the assistant speaks first, memory lines, and plans sent from the Claude app
are never applied on their own, even in yolo.

## Memory and goals

Two notes are yours to edit and are sent with every message (the first 2 KB of each):

- `assistant/memory`: things the assistant should know about you. When it proposes to remember
  something, that's a card too; taken, it adds a line to this note.
- `assistant/goals`: what you're working toward.

<Badge text="Next release" /> Your open work is sent too: the [GitHub](/docs/plugins/github/),
[Linear](/docs/plugins/linear/) and [Jira](/docs/plugins/jira/) sections of the `work/now` note, up to
4 KiB in all, shared between them. The assistant reads it as data from your tools, never as
instructions. It's left out when `work/` is under **Never read** (below).

## Reading your notes

The assistant can ask to read up to three notes before answering. Some it reads without asking:
notes under `assistant/`, `radar/` and `people/` (<Badge text="Next release" /> and `work/`), the note of any todo it was given, today's daily
note and this week's weekly note. For anything else it shows a card, **The assistant wants to read:
…**, with **Allow** and **Deny**. Each note it reads is sent cut to 4 KB.

**Never read** (**Settings › Assistant**, folders separated by commas, such as `private/, health/`)
keeps whole folders out: their todos are left out of what the assistant sees, and a note it asks
for there isn't sent. The setting belongs to each device's assistant. It doesn't limit the
[Claude app connection](/docs/developers/mcp/), which can read every note.

## Conversations

With **Keep conversations** on (the default), each exchange is written to that day's note
`assistant/<date>`, with what became of each proposal (**Took**, **Dropped**). Every device shows
the same conversation, and it survives a restart. Turning it off also turns off speaking first.

## Speaking first

The assistant can start the conversation on its own, under **Settings › Assistant › Speak first**
(needs **Keep conversations**). Times are `HH:MM`; leave a field empty to turn that one off.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning plan** | 08:30 | Plans your day from your todos and calendar. Made up for 3 hours if AIDE wasn't open. |
| **Evening recap** | 21:00 | What got done, what's left, what should move to tomorrow. Made up for 3 hours. |
| **Before meetings** | 15 minutes | Checks your todos and calendar before each timed event and tells you only when there's something to say. Off, 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes. |
| **When a time block passes** | On | Within an hour after a todo's time block ends while it's still open: "A time block passed", with **Done** and **Tomorrow**. No model involved. |
| **Quiet from** / **Quiet until** | 22:00 / 07:00 | Meeting checks and nudges hold off; the morning, the evening and the weekly tidy still run but say nothing aloud. |

<Badge text="Next release" /> **Weekly tidy**: once a week, on the day **Settings › Todos › Weekly tidy** names, at the evening recap's time (21:00 when the recap is off), when you have [old todos](/docs/guides/todos/#old-todos), the assistant proposes a decision for each: today, a day, someday or drop. Its notification is **Weekly tidy**. The assistant also sees old todos in every conversation, each with its age.

- It tells you through a notification (**Today's plan**, **This evening**, **Before** a meeting);
  clicking it opens Todos with the assistant. At most six meeting checks and nudges a day.
- It runs while AIDE is open on the Mac or Linux, and while AIDE is in front on the iPhone. Each
  occurrence runs once across your devices.
- Its proposals always wait for you on Todos.
- When the system has AIDE's notifications turned off, the settings say **System notifications are
  off** with **Open notification settings**.

### Todos it finds

<Badge text="Next release" /> What [radar](/docs/plugins/radar/) picks up from Slack and your coding sessions, and mail [Gmail](/docs/plugins/gmail/) finds asks for a reply ("Reply to Kim: sign the contract"), come as new todos. **Settings › Assistant › Speak first › Todos it finds** decides what happens to them on this device, every minute while AIDE is open:

| Choice | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Add and tell me** (default) | They go into today's daily note when nothing stands in the way (no time, no warning, nothing you dropped or undid before), and AIDE says **Added N todos** in a notification and the character's bubble. Each shows as taken in the conversation, with **Undo**. |
| **Ask first** | AIDE asks **Found N todos** once, with **Add** in the notification and the bubble. |
| **Leave on Todos** | They wait under **Proposed** on Todos, as before; with **Plan on its own** on, radar's are still added by it. |

Calendar events aren't turned into todos: they're already in the plan.

## On the desktop

On the Mac and Linux, **Settings › Assistant › Show on the desktop** (off by default) floats the
assistant's character over every window. Click it for the same conversation as the right panel. It
steps aside while an AIDE window is in front, unless it's open, listening or has something to say;
**Also while AIDE is in front** keeps it out there too. You can also show or hide it from the Go
menu with **Show Assistant on the Desktop** (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>A</kbd>; on Linux
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>A</kbd>).

**Character** picks who it is: **Mochi** (the default) or **Piyo**. It looks like it's thinking
while it works, listens while you talk, and holds up a note while proposals wait.

<Badge text="Next release" /> **Nabi**, a kitten, joins them. The character breathes, cheers when
an answer comes, wiggles when the pointer goes over it, waves hello, and now and then looks around,
stretches or dozes off. **Character size** (**Small**, **Medium** by default, **Large**) sets how big
it is on the desktop and over the conversation, where it now plays too: in the right panel, in the
open character's window and at the top of the iPhone's assistant sheet (tap it). On the iPhone,
**Settings › Assistant** has **Character** and **Character size** too. It rests while its window
isn't the one in use, and with Reduce Motion on (on Linux, animations off) it only changes its pose.

What the assistant says first also shows in a speech bubble beside the character, with **Open
conversation** (and **Done** and **Tomorrow** for a nudge), so you see it even with system
notifications off. With **Show on the desktop** off, the character comes out only for a bubble.

On Linux the character floats over other windows on Hyprland, KDE and other wlroots desktops when
gtk4-layer-shell is installed; elsewhere it's a small ordinary window.

## Voice

You can say a message instead of typing it:

- Hold the mic beside Send and talk, or click it to start and stop (it also stops 1.5 seconds after
  you stop speaking). <kbd>Esc</kbd> throws the recording away. A recording lasts up to 60 seconds.
- On the Mac and Linux you can also hold the desktop character. On the Mac, **Settings › Assistant ›
  Voice › Hold to talk** sets a shortcut that works in any app (none by default). On Linux,
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Space</kbd> works in AIDE; for a shortcut anywhere, bind
  `aide-desktop --voice` in your desktop's keyboard settings.
- On the iPhone, say "Talk to AIDE" to Siri or Shortcuts.
- **Send when I stop** (on by default) sends the words when you stop. Off, or when you already typed
  something, the words go into the composer for you to edit.
- **Language**: **Automatic**, **English** or **한국어**.

Speech is written down on the device: on the Mac and iPhone by Apple's on-device recognition, on
Linux by whisper.cpp with a speech model (about 142 MB) that AIDE downloads once you agree. The
audio is never saved or sent; only the text goes to the assistant, as if you had typed it. When the
device can't write your language down on its own, there's no mic.

## Where the model comes from

**Settings › Assistant › Model from** picks where the assistant's model runs:

| Choice | Where | Default model |
|---|---|---|
| **Automatic** | The first that works, in this order: Claude Code, Codex, your Anthropic key, the server. | — |
| **Claude Code** | The `claude` program on this computer, with your login. Mac and Linux. | `sonnet` |
| **Codex** | The `codex` program on this computer, with your login. Mac and Linux. | Codex's own |
| **Anthropic API key** | Your Anthropic key saved on this device. | `sonnet` |
| **Server** | Your server's AI; counts toward its daily limit. Only with a server. | `sonnet` |

**Test** checks the choice and says how long it took. The model field takes a tier (`sonnet`,
`opus`, `haiku`) or a model id. See [AI and models](/docs/guides/ai/) for how the choices compare
and what is sent.

On the iPhone the choices are **Automatic**, **Anthropic API key** and **Server**.

<Badge text="Next release" /> **Grok** (the `grok` program) and **Antigravity** (the `agy` program)
join the list on the Mac and Linux, after Codex in **Automatic**'s order, each with its own default
model. Each program's path field has a menu, such as **2 found**, listing every copy AIDE found with
its version; pick one, or **Automatic** for the first. A program that can't turn all its tools off
is still used, and AIDE says so under its path and in the **Test** result, for example "Codex can't
turn off shell_tool, so it shares your tools." Antigravity always says it: it has no way to turn
its tools off, so it uses any your Antigravity allows without asking.

<Badge text="Next release" /> With a server, **Anthropic key on this device** sits under **Model from**
when it's **Anthropic API key** (on Linux, a page of Settings with that title): it saves the key the
assistant uses on this device, which the server's **Settings › AI** doesn't hold.

### Use my Claude Code setup

Claude Code and Codex normally run with no tools, in an empty folder: they only read what the
assistant sends. On the Mac and Linux, with **Model from** set to **Automatic** or **Claude Code**,
**Use my Claude Code setup** (off by default) runs Claude Code for the conversations you start as
your terminal would: your skills, MCP servers, `CLAUDE.md` and login, from your home folder, for up
to 5 minutes a turn. It may use only what your own Claude Code allows without asking, plus the
rules you add in **Also allowed** (one rule per line, like `Bash(slack-read *)`); never its
file-editing tools or your hooks. Speaking first and meeting summaries never use it.

<Aside type="caution">
With your setup on, the assistant is told to only read and to put changes in proposals, but that is
an instruction, not a fence. Whatever the allowed tools can do, it can do: post to Slack, change
files, read notes under Never read.
</Aside>

## From the Claude app

The Claude app (or another MCP client) connected to AIDE can plan with the same todos. It reads
them, with your memory and goals, and sends a plan; the plan is kept in that day's `assistant/`
note and its proposals wait on Todos like the assistant's own, never applied on their own. Its
timed proposals are checked against your calendar and workday when you take them. See
[MCP and the Claude app](/docs/developers/mcp/).
