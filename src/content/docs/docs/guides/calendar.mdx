The Calendar section shows your events next to your notes and todos, so you can plan a day in one
place and start a meeting note from an event. The same events appear under **Todos › Today** and
feed the [assistant](/docs/guides/assistant/) when it plans your day. AIDE reads calendars; it
doesn't create or edit events.

## Where the events come from

Which calendars you see depends on how you run AIDE.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="With a server">
When your notes are on a desktop host or your own server, the Calendar section shows your **Google
calendars**, brought in by the [Calendar plugin](/docs/plugins/gcal/) running beside the server. It
syncs every five minutes and keeps last week through the next six weeks. Until the plugin has
connected, the Calendar section says **Calendar isn't connected**.

Every device sees the same calendars and the same choices: which calendars show, their colours and
which ones remind you are saved on the server.
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Mac and iPhone, no server">
With notes kept on the device and no server, AIDE reads the calendars of the Mac or iPhone itself
(the ones in Apple's Calendar app, including any accounts you added there). The first time, the
system asks whether AIDE may read them. If you said no, the section shows **Calendar access is off**:
allow AIDE in System Settings (Settings on the iPhone) › **Privacy & Security** › **Calendars**.

These events stay on the device. AIDE shows 60 days back and a year ahead. Which calendars show and
their colours are saved on that device only.
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Linux, no server">
Without a server, the Linux app reads iCalendar (`.ics`) calendars you add yourself, by web
address (`https://` or `webcal://`) or as a file on the computer:

1. Open **Settings › Calendars**.
2. Under **Add a calendar**, type a **Name** and the **Web address or file**, then choose **Add**.

Web calendars refresh every 30 minutes and when you open the Calendar section after 15 minutes or
more; files refresh whenever they change. A calendar may be up to 10 MB. The list is also kept in
`config.toml` under `[[calendar.sources]]`, so you can edit it by hand. To rename or remove one,
open its row in **Settings › Calendars** (**Remove this calendar**).

These calendars are read-only and stay on the computer.
</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Views

Choose a view at the top: **Schedule**, **Day**, **Week** or **Month**. AIDE remembers the one you
used last.

- **Schedule** lists the days from the one you're on to the end of what's synced.
- **Day** and **Week** lay the events out on a 24-hour timeline. Click a day in Week to open it in
  Day.
- **Month** shows a grid; click a day to open it in Day.

**Today** jumps back to today, and the arrows beside it move one day, week or month. You can also
swipe sideways: with a finger on the iPhone, with two fingers on a Mac or Linux trackpad. The
**Daily note** button in the header opens today's daily note.

When the server's copy hasn't been synced for more than 15 minutes, a warning beside the arrows
says when it last was, for example **Synced 2 hours ago**.

## Choose calendars, colours and reminders

The **Calendars** button opens the list of your calendars, grouped as **My calendars** and **Other
calendars**. For each one:

- the switch shows or hides it;
- the coloured dot picks its colour: one of Google Calendar's colours, **Custom**, or back to the
  original with **Use Google's color** (**Use the calendar's color** for a device or `.ics`
  calendar);
- the bell (with a server only) turns reminders for that calendar on or off.

A calendar that failed to sync says **Sync failed** with the reason.

### Reminders and prep

With a server, the same list has a **Reminders** section. These run on the server, so they reach
you even when no app is open:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Remind before events** | On | Sends a reminder before each timed event in a calendar with the bell on. |
| **When** | 10 min before | 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes before. |
| **AI prep notes in the daily note** | On | After the reminder, the server's AI looks through your notes for anything related and writes a short prep note into the day's note. |
| **Tell me when there's something to prepare** | Off | Also notifies you with that prep, but only when the AI found something worth telling you. |

The prep uses the AI set in **Settings › AI** on the server ([AI and models](/docs/guides/ai/)).
The [assistant](/docs/guides/assistant/#speaking-first) can also check before meetings, on your
computer; with both turned on, you may hear about a meeting twice.

Calendars read from the device or from `.ics` files have no bells and no reminders of AIDE's own:
the Mac and iPhone alert you through the system's Calendar as usual.

## An event's details

Click an event to open it. Under its title you see when it is, where, who is attending and which
calendar it's in, then its description. The toolbar has:

- **Meeting note**: opens the meeting note for this event, and makes it the first time.
- **Join video call**: when the event has a video call link.
- **Open in Google Calendar** for a Google event, or **Open link** for a device calendar's event
  when it has a link.

### Meeting notes from events

**Meeting note** makes a note under `meetings/` named after the day and the event, for example
`meetings/2026-10-17-design-review`, with the event's title, start, end and attendees. It also
lists notes that look related (the previous meeting in the same series, notes linked from the
description, notes titled like an attendee, and search hits for the title), adds the meeting to
that day's daily note, and gathers a word list (names, product words) to help the transcription.
A repeating event gets one note per occurrence, and the same occurrence always opens the same note,
even if the event moves.

For a Google event, the Calendar plugin also fills the note's **Context** section with what the
calendar knows (when, where, the video link, attendees, the full description) and updates it while
the event changes, until 30 days after the meeting.

From there you can record and sum up the meeting: see [Meetings](/docs/guides/meetings/).
