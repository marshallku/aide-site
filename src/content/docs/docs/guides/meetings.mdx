A meeting in AIDE is a note. You make it from a calendar event or on the spot, record the meeting
into it, and AIDE writes down what was said on your device, sums it up with your assistant's model,
and proposes action items that become todos once you approve them. The meeting also gets a line in
that day's daily note.

## Make a meeting note

- **From an event:** open the event in [Calendar](/docs/guides/calendar/) and choose **Meeting
  note**. The note gets the event's title, time and attendees, links to related notes, and a word
  list for transcription. Choosing it again opens the same note.
- **On the spot:** **File › New Meeting** on the Mac (<kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>R</kbd>),
  **New meeting** in the Wiki's **More** menu on the iPhone, or **New meeting** in the command
  palette on Linux. This makes a meeting starting now, opens it and starts recording.

Meeting notes live under `meetings/`, named after the day and the title, for example
`meetings/2026-10-17-design-review`. You can rename or move the note: AIDE finds a meeting by an id
in its frontmatter, not by its path.

A meeting note has these sections:

| Section | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Links to the notes the meeting is about; for a Google event, what the calendar knows. |
| **Notes** | Yours. Write anything here (or anywhere outside the generated parts). |
| **Summary** | The summary and decisions, once it's summed up. |
| **Actions** | The action items you approved, as todos. |
| **Transcript** | What was said, as written down, with its corrections. |

The generated parts are kept in their own blocks, and AIDE rewrites only those, so your own writing
is never touched. Text that came from elsewhere (the transcript, an event's description) sits in a
code block, so nothing in it turns into a todo.

## Record

On a meeting note, choose **Record**. A bar over the note shows the elapsed time, the sources and a
level meter, with **Pause**/**Resume** and **Stop**. While a meeting records, every window shows a
small pill with its title and time that opens the note.

- **Sources:** the microphone, and on the Mac and Linux also what the computer plays, so the other
  side of a call is recorded too. That's **Settings › Meetings › Record what this Mac plays**
  (**Record what this computer plays** on Linux), on by default; the Mac asks for permission once.
  Recording starts with whichever sources work and says which.
- **Record again** after Stop adds to the same recording; the timeline keeps counting.
- One meeting records at a time. While it does, the assistant's mic is off.
- Losing the microphone pauses the recording. If the disk fills up or writing falls behind, it
  stops and keeps what it has. After a crash, AIDE recovers the recording at the next launch.

## Import a recording

To take in audio recorded elsewhere (Voice Memos, a recorder, a call recording, anything the device
can play, up to 6 hours):

- On a meeting note, **Import recording…** adds the file to that meeting.
- **File › Import Recording…** on the Mac, **Import recording** in the Wiki's **More** menu on the
  iPhone, or the command palette on Linux asks which meeting first: AIDE guesses when the file was
  recorded (**Recorded around**, which you can change) and lists the events around then, or **A new
  meeting** named after the file.

An imported file is written down and summed up like a live recording.

## Written down on your device

What was said is written down on the device that holds the recording, in 30-second pieces as the
recording goes, by the same engines as the assistant's voice: Apple's on-device speech recognition
on the Mac and iPhone, whisper.cpp on Linux. Nothing is sent anywhere for this. The language is the
one in **Settings › Assistant › Voice › Language**.

Each meeting has a word list (`meeting-glossary` in its frontmatter): attendees, titles of the linked
notes, and names from the event's title and description. The recognizer is told to expect those
words. Add your own to the list to help with names and jargon.

When it can't write a meeting down (no engine for the language, permission refused, on Linux the
speech model not downloaded), the note says **Not written down yet** with the reason and
**Retry**. On Linux, get the speech model under **Settings › Assistant › Voice**.

## Summed up

Once the recording has stopped and is fully written down, AIDE sums it up on its own with the
assistant's model (**Settings › Assistant › Model from**; see [AI and models](/docs/guides/ai/)).
The model gets the title, start and attendees, the word list, the start of each linked note, the
calendar's context and the transcript; a long meeting is summed up in parts.

The note then gets:

- **Summary**: a few bullets, where it was summed up ("Summed up with Claude Code (sonnet)"), and
  **Decisions**, each with the time it came up.
- **Corrections**: words the model thinks were misheard, based on the attendees and your notes,
  shown in the transcript as the corrected word with what was heard.
- **Proposed action items**, waiting for you.

If the transcript or the context changes afterwards, the note says **Changed since it was summed
up.** with **Process again**. A failure says **Not summed up** with the reason and **Process
again**.

## Approve action items

Under a summed-up meeting, each proposed action item shows its text, who it's for and when it came
up, with **Approve** and **Dismiss**, and **Approve all** when there are several.

- **Approve** adds a todo to the **Actions** section, such as `- [ ] Send the report [meeting:: a1]`,
  so it shows in [Todos](/docs/guides/todos/) like any other.
- **Dismiss** drops it.
- AIDE remembers every item it ever proposed, so an item you dismissed or edited isn't proposed
  again.
- With the assistant set to **Apply right away (yolo)**, new items are approved as soon as they're
  written.

**Transcript** opens the whole transcript with the corrections applied; **Undo** beside a correction
takes it back.

## Keep recordings

**Settings › Meetings › Keep recordings** decides how long the audio stays on the device:

- **7 days after processing** (the default)
- **Right after processing**
- **Until deleted**

Audio is deleted only after the meeting is summed up and the time has passed. The transcript in the
note stays either way.

## Privacy

<Aside type="note">
A meeting's audio never leaves the device that recorded it. It is kept in AIDE's own data folder on
that device (Application Support on the Mac and iPhone, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/aide` on Linux), and only
the text goes into the note.
</Aside>

Summing up does send meeting content to a model: the transcript, the attendees, the word list and
the start of the linked notes go to the backend in **Settings › Assistant › Model from**. Notes
under the assistant's **Never read** folders are never sent. Without a model, recording and the
transcript still work; the meeting just isn't summed up.
