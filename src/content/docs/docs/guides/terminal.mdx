On the Mac and Linux, AIDE has a terminal of its own, so you can keep a shell next to your notes. On
the iPhone, which has no local shell, the SSH section opens terminals to your servers instead.

## Terminal on the Mac and Linux

The **Terminal** section in the sidebar is a row of tabs over a shell, filling the main area.

- **+** or **New tab** opens a shell in a new tab (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> on the Mac,
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> on Linux), in the folder of the tab you're in, or your
  home folder. It starts as a login shell, so your profile runs.
- Each tab is titled by what the shell sets, else the program's name. **Rename…** in a tab's menu
  gives it your own name; **Close** or its × ends the shell.
- With no tab open, the section says **No shells open**.
- The shells keep running while you're elsewhere in AIDE. Coming back finds them as you left them.
  <Badge text="Next release" /> They keep running after you quit AIDE too: see
  [Shells that outlive AIDE](#shells-that-outlive-aide-).

### Split a tab into panes <Badge text="Next release" />

A tab can hold more than one shell side by side. Each part is a pane, with its own shell, and you
split and move between them from the keyboard or the **Go** menu:

| Action | Mac | Linux |
|---|---|---|
| **Split Right** | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> |
| **Split Down** | <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>E</kbd> |
| **Next Pane** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> |
| **Previous Pane** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Left</kbd> |
| Close the focused pane | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> |

- The keys work only while a terminal pane has the keyboard. **Go** › **Split Right**,
  **Split Down**, **Next Pane** and **Previous Pane** (on the Mac) are greyed out otherwise.
- A split halves the focused pane. The new pane opens a shell in the focused pane's folder.
- Click a pane to focus it. While a tab has more than one pane, the focused one has an outline in
  the accent colour.
- Drag the line between two panes to resize them.
- Each pane keeps at least 20 columns and 5 rows. A split that would leave less is refused with a
  beep.
- The tab's title, its folder (where a new tab starts) and the find bar follow the focused pane.
- <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> on Linux) closes the focused
  pane and ends its shell; on the tab's last pane it closes the tab. The tab's × and **Close** still
  close the whole tab, with every pane in it.

### Beside your notes

The right panel (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd> on
Linux) has a **Terminal** view: the same tabs and the same shells, next to whatever the main area
shows, such as a note. While the main area is the Terminal section, the shells are there and the
panel says so.

Find <Badge text="Next release" /> goes on past the last match to the first, and says **No matches**
when there are none, on the Mac and Linux.

For the tab, pane, find and text-size keys, see [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#terminal).

### Shells that outlive AIDE <Badge text="Next release" />

Your shells run in AIDE's terminal server, a small program of its own, not in the app. Quitting
AIDE, closing its window or AIDE closing unexpectedly leaves them running, with whatever runs in
them: a build, an SSH session, an agent. When you open AIDE again, each tab and pane is back as you
left it, still running, with its screen and scrollback.

- On the Mac the terminal server comes inside the app and runs as a login item (it can show under
  **System Settings › General › Login Items**). On Linux it's installed beside the app and runs as
  the `aide-mux.service` systemd user service when your session has a user manager; otherwise AIDE
  starts it on its own.
- Each vault has its own set of tabs on the server. On the Mac, two windows of the same vault show
  the same tabs.
- The first time you quit with shells open, AIDE says once that **Your shells keep running**, and
  where to turn that off.
- On the Mac, while AIDE reaches the server, the Terminal section says **Starting the terminal…**.
  If the server can't be reached, it says **The terminal server isn't running**, with the reason and
  **Try again**.
- A server left from an older (or newer) AIDE that this AIDE can't talk to says so: **The terminal
  server is from an older AIDE**, with **Restart it**. Restarting asks first
  (**Restart the terminal server?**), since its shells and the programs in them end.
- <Badge text="Next release" /> After AIDE updates itself, the terminal server that was running
  keeps running (your shells with it) until you restart it. A bar over the tabs says **The terminal
  server is from an older AIDE (1.0.10). Restart it to use this version's.**, with **Restart it**,
  which asks first as above, and a close that puts the bar away until you quit AIDE. Nothing restarts
  by itself. On Linux, if the update couldn't put the new terminal server in place, the bar says
  **This version's terminal server couldn't be installed** with the reason, and offers no restart.
  While the update is still being installed, it says so, and **Restart it** appears once it's done.
- With no AIDE window attached, the server keeps running as long as it has shells. It stops by
  itself only after 7 days with no window and no shell running.

To have quitting end your shells instead, turn off **Settings › Terminal › Keep shells running
after AIDE quits**. Then:

- Quitting AIDE asks first when a program other than the shell is running in a pane: **End the
  running programs?** lists them, with **Quit** and **Cancel**. On the Mac, closing the last window
  of a vault asks the same, with **Close**; on Linux, closing the window asks.
- After you quit, the server ends the shells of the vaults this AIDE showed, unless another AIDE
  (on another computer, say) still has them open. The layout is kept, and the next start opens it
  with new shells.
- A server with no window for 24 hours ends its shells and stops.

Changes reach open tabs at once, except where the table says new tabs. On Linux the same settings
are also kept in `config.toml` under `[terminal]` (`kitty_keyboard`, `images`, `reopen_tabs`,
`keep_running`). <Badge text="Next release" /> **Scrollback**, **Shell**, **Kitty keyboard protocol**,
**Reopen tabs at launch** and **Keep shells running after AIDE quits** are also the terminal
server's own: a change on one AIDE reaches the server, and the last change wins.

### Tabs after a restart <Badge text="Next release" />

The terminal server keeps each vault's layout: its tabs with their names, the splits at their sizes,
the tab you were on, and the folder each pane's shell is in. When the server starts again, after
your computer restarts say, the layout comes back with a new shell in each pane, in its folder.
Programs that were running, the scrollback and the environment don't come back; a folder that's gone
opens at home instead.

- The layout is saved a second after each change, a `cd` included.
- A tab you closed stays closed.
- Panes attached to a remote comux aren't kept.
- When you close every tab, the next start opens one fresh shell.
- Turning **Reopen tabs at launch** off forgets the saved layouts.
- The first time the new AIDE starts, the tabs an earlier AIDE had saved are taken over.

### Keys and pictures <Badge text="Next release" />

Keys with no text, such as <kbd>Home</kbd>, <kbd>F5</kbd> or the arrows, reach programs the way
xterm sends them. A program that asks for the kitty keyboard protocol gets every key with its
modifiers instead, while **Kitty keyboard protocol** is on.

On Linux, dropping files on a tab types their paths, quoted for the shell. A dropped image from
another app is saved first and its path typed; a dropped link is typed as text.

The terminal sets `TERM=xterm-256color`, `COLORTERM=truecolor` and `TERM_PROGRAM=AIDE`. In the
release, it otherwise passes on the environment AIDE was started with. <Badge text="Next release" />
The terminal server starts each shell with your login shell's environment instead, read once when
the server starts, so a shell never carries variables of the session AIDE happened to start in. A
program in the terminal can copy to your clipboard but can't read it.

<Badge text="Next release" /> Each pane's shell also gets `COPAD_SOCKET` and `AIDE_TERMINAL_SOCKET`,
the terminal server's [terminal socket](/docs/developers/local-api/#terminal-socket), and
`COPAD_PANEL_ID` and `AIDE_MUX_PANE`, that pane's id. With them, clicking comux's desktop
notification brings forward the exact tab and pane it came from, and copad's `coctl` keeps working.
[`aidectl terminal`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal) uses the same socket to read, type into,
split and list panes and tabs from a script.

### comux in the terminal <Badge text="Next release" />

On the Mac and on Linux, a tab running comux shows comux's sessions, spaces and agents in AIDE's own
controls around the terminal, instead of comux drawing them as text:

- A sidebar on the left: **Spaces** and **Agents**, each agent with its status (**working**,
  **ready**, **blocked**, **idle** or **done**). Click a row to go there; rest the pointer on one to
  preview its pane. Drag its edge to change its width; AIDE remembers it.
- A top bar: where you are, the **Work graph** buttons and the bell. The bell shows how many
  notifications are unread. Click it for **Notifications**: click one to go to its pane, **Dismiss**
  one, or **Clear all**.
- A status bar at the bottom: the session, its tabs, counters (**Bells**, **Waiting for you**,
  **Agents running**, **Unread notifications**), usage gauges and the clock.
- comux's pickers, prompts and confirms as a card over the tab. comux's own menus are a card too on
  Linux; on the Mac they open as a regular menu at the pointer. Right-click a row or a chip for its
  menu, such as **Jump to pane**, **Rename tab…** or **Rename session…**.

**Work graph** opens a graph of every agent's work beside the terminal; **Every agent's work, across
sessions** widens it to all sessions. The buttons never take the keyboard, so what you type keeps
going to the terminal.

To use a comux on a server, right-click a tab and choose **Open remote comux…**. Enter a host from
`~/.ssh/config` or `user@host`, and the comux command there if it isn't `comux`. It opens in a new
tab over `ssh`, with that server's sessions in the sidebar. <Badge text="Next release" /> It is also **Open remote comux…** in the command palette, an action (`terminal/open-remote-comux`) you can give a shortcut in [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/guides/shortcuts/).

Only the tab on screen (in the Terminal section or the right panel) gets AIDE's controls. A tab you
can't see, or one without comux, shows comux as comux draws it itself. In a tab split into panes,
each pane runs its own comux, and the focused pane's comux gets the controls.

### Open remote AIDE… <Badge text="Next release" />

Open another computer's AIDE terminal sessions in this window, beside your own tabs, over SSH. The
other computer can be a Mac or Linux machine where AIDE is installed and has been opened once.

1. Right-click a tab and choose **Open remote AIDE…**, or run **Open remote AIDE…** from the
   command palette (the action `terminal/open-remote-aide`, which you can give a shortcut in
   [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/guides/shortcuts/)).
2. Enter a host from `~/.ssh/config` or `user@host`, or pick one of your `~/.ssh/config` hosts
   under the field, and choose **Connect**.
3. **Sessions on** the host (**Sessions on box**, say) lists its sessions with their tab count and tab names, and whether one
   is already open in this window or another. Pick one.

The session's tabs open after your own, each with a globe and the host's name before its title.

- What you type, splits, new tabs (**+** or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>T</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>T</kbd>
  on Linux, while one of its tabs is selected), renames and closes happen on that computer, in that
  session. The shells are that computer's, with its environment.
- A session is open in one window at most. Opening it again brings that window and its tabs
  forward.
- **End session…**, beside a session in the list, ends its shells on that computer, after asking.
- **Disconnect from** the host (**Disconnect from box**), in a remote tab's menu, closes its tabs here. Closing the window or
  quitting AIDE does the same. None of these ends anything on the other computer, whatever **Keep
  shells running after AIDE quits** says: its shells end only when you close a remote tab or pane
  with its × or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> on Linux), or
  end the session.
- Remote tabs aren't part of this computer's layout and don't come back by themselves. The next
  window offers what was open (**demo on box was open here**, with **Reconnect**); AIDE never connects
  by itself, since SSH may need your key unlocked.

If the connection drops, the tabs keep their last screen under **Disconnected from box** (the host's
name), with **Reconnect**. Reconnecting shows the session as it is now, splits, renames and new tabs another
AIDE made meanwhile included, with each pane's shell still running. A session that ended meanwhile
says so (**demo isn't on box any more**), with **Close**.

A pane shown on two computers has one size: the one you typed or clicked in last sizes it. On the
other one the pane keeps that size, cut off or with empty space around it, and says who sizes it in its
corner (**Sized by mac-mini**). Clicking or typing in the pane gives it this window's size again. This
works the same for your own panes when another AIDE opens them.

SSH is your system's `ssh`, with your own keys, agent and `~/.ssh/config`. AIDE never asks for a
password and never relaxes host key checking:

- A host whose key isn't in `known_hosts` is refused: connect to it once with `ssh` yourself to
  check and accept its key.
- A host whose key changed is refused with `ssh`'s own warning.
- A host where AIDE hasn't been set up says **AIDE isn't set up on box: open AIDE there once**.

The other computer treats the connection as remote: none of your local environment reaches its
shells, and nothing running there can bring windows forward on this computer.

### Coding agents <Badge text="Next release" />

AIDE knows when a coding agent runs in a pane, what it's doing, and when it finishes or needs you. You
see it in the Agents list, on its tab and in the status items, and you get a notification when you
aren't looking at it.

AIDE recognises the program in front in a pane by its name: Claude Code (`claude`), Codex (`codex`),
and `agy`, `grok`, `aider`, `cursor`, `gemini`, `opencode`, `droid`, `copilot`, `qwen` and `crush`.
Claude Code's state comes from the session file it keeps; every other agent's is read from what's on
its screen. For Claude Code and Codex, AIDE also reads their own logs for the step they're on (the
command they run, the file they edit) and how full their context is. Nothing is sent anywhere to do
this, and no program is started.

An agent is in one of these states:

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Working** | It is busy with a turn. |
| **Done** | Its turn ended while you weren't looking. It becomes **Waiting** once you look at the pane. |
| **Waiting** | Its turn ended and you've seen it; it waits for your next prompt. |
| **Needs you** | It asks you something: a permission or a question. |
| **Idle** | An agent is in front, and nothing says what it's doing. |

#### The Agents list

The Terminal section has a panel of its own, beside the tabs, which **Toggle Panel** shows and hides
as for your notes. Under **Agents** it lists the agents in this window's tabs, with the number that
need you beside the heading. Each row shows the agent's state and how long it has been in it, the
tab's name, and under it what it asks while it needs you, else the step it's on, else the last line
it printed, with a small gauge of its context when AIDE knows it.

- The agents that need you come first, then the rest in tab order.
- A remote session's agents (see [Open remote AIDE…](#open-remote-aide-)) are listed under the
  host's name.
- A row that says **Not updating** is one AIDE couldn't read just now; it shows the last state it
  knows.
- Click a row to go to that agent: its tab is selected, its pane gets the keyboard and the window
  comes forward. Looking at it this way turns **Done** into **Waiting**.
- With none, the list says **No agents running**.

A tab with an agent that is working, done or needs you shows a small mark next to its name: a dot
while it works, a check when it's done, and a warning sign while it needs you. Point at the mark to
read the agent and its state.

The [status items](/docs/guides/appearance/#status-items-mac-and-linux-) at the bottom of the sidebar
also count the agents that need you or are done.

#### Notifications

When an agent finishes or needs you, AIDE posts a notification, **Claude finished · build** or
**Claude needs you · build** (the agent, then its tab), with what it asks when it's waiting on you, Codex's last message when it
finishes, or else just **Turn finished** or **Waiting for you**. There is
one notification for each time, however many AIDE windows show the pane, and none while you're
looking at the pane: AIDE in front with that pane on screen. A notification for the same pane
replaces the one before.

Click it to go to the pane: AIDE selects its tab, gives the pane the keyboard, brings the window
forward and marks the agent as seen. A pane closed since says **That terminal has closed**.

The first notification asks for permission on the Mac. On Linux the desktop may keep the window from
coming forward; the pane is selected all the same.

#### Settings › Terminal › Agents

These are the terminal server's own settings, so every AIDE that uses it shows the same: a change on
one reaches the server, and the last change wins. On Linux they're also kept in `config.toml` under
`[terminal]`.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Watch for coding agents** | On | Off, AIDE doesn't look for agents at all: the list stays empty, and there are no marks, status item or notifications (`agents`). |
| **Notify when an agent finishes** | On | A notification when an agent's turn ends (`notify_done`). Off, it still shows as **Done**. |
| **Notify when an agent needs you** | On | A notification when an agent asks you something (`notify_blocked`). |
| **Remind after** | 10 min | **Off**, 5, 10 or 30 min: one more notification that long after an agent started needing you, if you haven't looked at it (`notify_remind`). |
| **Send to your phone** | On | The same notifications on phones you connect, sealed so only they can read them (`push`). This is for the iPhone app and isn't available yet: until a phone is connected, nothing is sent. |
| **Set up agent hooks…** | | Below. |

If AIDE stops being able to read agents' files (a read that never returned), the group says **Agent
detection stopped: a file read didn't return. Restart the terminal server to try again.** The agents
it knows still follow what's on their screens.

#### Set up agent hooks…

Without hooks, AIDE notices an agent finishing or needing you within about a second, and waits a few
seconds after a turn ends to be sure. With them, Claude Code and Codex tell AIDE the moment it
happens, and Claude Code also reports how full its context is.

**Set up agent hooks…** shows what it would change first, under **Set up agent hooks?**: each file,
where it links to when it's a symlink, and the lines that change. Nothing is written until you choose
**Apply**.

- Claude Code's `~/.claude/settings.json` gets a `Stop` and a `Notification` hook, and its
  `statusLine` runs through AIDE first. A status line you already have is kept and still runs, with
  the same output.
- Codex's `~/.codex/config.toml` (or `$CODEX_HOME/config.toml`) gets AIDE in its `notify` program,
  which then runs the one you had.
- A tool that isn't set up on this computer is skipped. Running it again says **Nothing to change.**
- Each file is backed up beside itself first (`settings.json.aide-backup-<time>`), and a file that
  changed since you saw the diff isn't written.

The hooks only do anything in AIDE's own panes. Elsewhere, or inside comux, they run what you had
and nothing else. [`aidectl agent install-hooks`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent) does the same from a
terminal, and takes them out again with `--remove`.

#### Hold Claude Code's auto-compaction until you commit

AIDE can hold Claude Code's automatic compaction while the work tree has uncommitted changes to
tracked files, so a compacted conversation doesn't lose track of work that isn't committed yet.
Claude carries on uncompacted and asks again before its next request. It's added only with
[`aidectl agent install-hooks --pre-compact`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent), as a `PreCompact`
hook.

- Claude Code compacts close to the end of its context by default, too late for this. Set
  `CLAUDE_AUTOCOMPACT_PCT_OVERRIDE` (the percentage of the context at which it compacts, such as
  `70`) in the environment Claude Code runs in. AIDE doesn't set it.
- It holds only an automatic compaction, never `/compact`; only while there's room left (60,000
  tokens beyond a tenth of the context); and at most 40 times in a row. Untracked files don't count.
- Whenever it isn't sure (no AIDE pane, an old reading, `git` slow or failing, not a repository), it
  lets Claude compact. It answers within a second and a half.

## SSH on the iPhone

<Aside type="caution">
The iPhone app is in a TestFlight beta and isn't public yet.
</Aside>

SSH is a built-in feature you turn on in **Settings › Core plugins › SSH** ("Saved hosts,
keys and passwords, terminals to your servers."). It adds an **SSH** section: open sessions on top,
then your saved hosts by group, with a search field.

On an iPad at full width, **SSH** is on the ribbon, where the Mac has the
terminal.

### Add a host

Choose **+** (**Add host**) and fill in:

- **Label**: what the list shows, such as "My server".
- **Host** (`example.com` or `192.168.0.2`), **Port** (22 by default) and **User**.
- **Group**, to sort hosts in the list (optional).
- **Sign in with**: **A password, asked each time**, or a key or saved password from your keychain.
  **Manage keychain…** opens the keychain.
- **Startup command**, such as `tmux attach`: offered in the session's menu as **Run startup
  command**, never run on its own.

Tap a host to connect. The session has its own terminal with the keyboard's accessory row, and a
menu with **Run startup command**, **Disconnect** and **Reconnect**. Sessions stay open while you
move around the app, and switching between notes on the device and a server doesn't close them;
turning SSH off does.

### comux on a host <Badge text="Next release" />

If a host runs comux, its row in the list has a **comux** button beside it. Tapping the row still
opens a shell; **comux** opens the host's comux instead, over the same sign-in, known hosts and
keychain. Touching and holding the row offers both: **Open a shell** and **Open comux**. An open
comux is listed with the sessions as "comux on" the host; swipe it to **Close** it.

The comux screen shows the panes as comux draws them, with:

- comux's tabs across the top, and a button on the left that opens **Spaces and agents** as a
  drawer.
- **Notifications** on the right, with the unread count, as a drawer from the right.
- comux's status line at the bottom, and its pickers, prompts, confirms and menus over the screen.
- A key bar over the keyboard: **esc**, **tab**, **ctrl**, the arrows, comux's keys (**^b**,
  **^f**, **^b a**, **^b c**, **^b n**, **^b p**, **^b z**), **^c** and **Hide**.
- The **Session** menu: **Work graph** (a graph of every agent's work, in a sheet), **New tab**,
  **New session…**, **Resume a conversation…**, **Reconnect** and **Disconnect**.

In the host's editor, the **comux** section has **comux command**: what runs on the host for comux.
Left empty, AIDE runs `comux proxy --stdio` from your login shell.

For a host that signs in with **A password, asked each time**, the comux screen asks for the
password. If the server turns it down, it says so and asks again.

### AIDE sessions on a host <Badge text="Next release" />

If AIDE is installed on a host (a Mac or Linux machine where you've opened AIDE once), its row in the
list has an **AIDE** button beside **comux**. It opens that computer's AIDE terminal sessions, the
same ones its own Terminal shows, over the same sign-in, known hosts and keychain as a shell.
Touching and holding the row offers **Open AIDE** too.

1. Tap **AIDE**. **AIDE on** the host (**AIDE on box**, say) lists its sessions, each with its name
   and tab count.
2. Pick a session. Its tabs run across the top, with **+** for **New tab**, and the tab you pick
   shows one pane at a time.
3. Type. What you type, paste and tap goes to that pane's shell on the other computer.

- A tab split into panes shows its focused pane; **Panes** in the bar lists the others to switch to.
  The iPhone shows splits other computers make, but doesn't split itself.
- The **Session** menu has **New tab**, **Rename tab…**, **Close pane** and **Reconnect**. **Close
  pane** asks first, and names the program still running there. There's no way to end a whole
  session from the iPhone.
- Leaving the screen or the app only lets go of the session: its shells keep running on the other
  computer. Coming back shows each pane as it is now.
- A pane has one size. Opening it on the iPhone doesn't change it: if a computer sized it last,
  the iPhone shows it at that size, cut off or with space around it, with **Sized by** that
  computer (**Sized by mac-mini**) and **Fit to this phone**. Typing, pasting, tapping into the
  pane or **Fit to this phone** gives it the iPhone's size until that computer takes it back.
- Programs that use the mouse get your taps as clicks and a two-finger drag as the scroll wheel.
- Pictures that programs draw in the terminal (kitty images) aren't shown on the iPhone.
- A paste larger than the pane can take now is refused whole: **That paste is too large to send**.
  When a program stops reading what you type, the pane says **Not responding** until it reads again.

While it connects, the screen says **Connecting to box…**. If the connection drops, it says
**Disconnected from box**, with **Reconnect**. A host where AIDE hasn't been set up says **AIDE isn't
set up on box: open AIDE there once**, and one whose AIDE is older than the app says **AIDE on box is
older than this app: update it there**.

In the host's editor, the **AIDE** section has **AIDE command**: what runs on the host for its
sessions. Left empty, AIDE runs the AIDE installed there.

### Agent notifications <Badge text="Next release" />

**Agent notifications**, in the host editor's **comux** section, is on by default. The next time you
open that host's comux, AIDE asks for permission to notify you and gives comux a key made for this
iPhone. comux then sends its agents' notifications through a push relay to Apple's push service,
sealed with that key: only this iPhone can read the title and text. The relay and Apple carry only
a placeholder. If comux turns notifications down, or is too old for them, the section says so.

Tapping a notification opens the SSH section on that host's comux, at the agent's pane. A
notification from the comux you're looking at doesn't show a banner. If its key is gone, the
notification reads "Agent notification" and "Open AIDE to see it." If SSH is turned off, tapping it
tells you to turn SSH on first.

Turning the switch off, or deleting the host, deletes its key at once. comux is told to forget this
iPhone the next time you connect to it.

### Keys and passwords

**Keychain** (in the section's menu) holds what you sign in with. Choose **Add**, then:

- **Password…**: a password saved under a name.
- **New ED25519 key…**: AIDE makes a key; its private half never leaves the iPhone's keychain. Open
  the key to **Copy public key** or **Share public key**, and add that line to
  `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` on the server.
- **Import a key…**: an ED25519 private key in OpenSSH format (`BEGIN OPENSSH PRIVATE KEY`),
  unencrypted or with a passphrase.
- **New Secure Enclave key…** <Badge text="Next release" />: where the iPhone or iPad has a Secure
  Enclave, it makes a P-256 key there. Its private half can't be read out, not even by AIDE, and it
  works only on that device. Copy or share its public key the same way.

Keys and passwords are kept in the iPhone's keychain, never in the host list.

### Known hosts

The first time you connect to a host, AIDE shows its key type and fingerprint. Check it with the
server's owner (`ssh-keygen -lf` on the server's host key) before **Trust and connect**. If a
host's key later changes, AIDE stops and shows both: someone may be intercepting the connection,
so choose **Replace the key and connect** only if you know the server was set up again.

**Known hosts** (in the section's menu) lists every key you've trusted; **Forget** removes one.
