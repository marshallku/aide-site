On the Mac and Linux, AIDE has a terminal of its own, so you can keep a shell next to your notes. On
the iPhone, which has no local shell, the SSH section opens terminals to your servers instead.

## Terminal on the Mac and Linux

The **Terminal** section in the sidebar is a row of tabs over a shell, filling the main area.
The tabs move into one bar under the shell, as in comux and tmux:
see [The status bar](#the-status-bar).

- **+** or **New tab** opens a shell in a new tab (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> on the Mac,
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> on Linux), in the folder of the tab you're in, or your
  home folder. It starts as a login shell, so your profile runs.
- Each tab is titled by what the shell sets, else the program's name. **Rename…** in a tab's menu
  gives it your own name; **Close** or its × ends the shell.
- With no tab open, the section says **No shells open**.
- The shells keep running while you're elsewhere in AIDE. Coming back finds them as you left them.
  They keep running after you quit AIDE too: see
  [Shells that outlive AIDE](#shells-that-outlive-aide).

### Split a tab into panes

A tab can hold more than one shell side by side. Each part is a pane, with its own shell, and you
split and move between them from the keyboard or the **Go** menu:

| Action | Mac | Linux |
|---|---|---|
| **Split Right** | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> |
| **Split Down** | <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>E</kbd> |
| **Next Pane** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> |
| **Previous Pane** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> | none |
| Close the focused pane | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> |

- The keys work only while a terminal pane has the keyboard. **Go** › **Split Right**,
  **Split Down**, **Next Pane** and **Previous Pane** (on the Mac) are greyed out otherwise.
- A split halves the focused pane. The new pane opens a shell in the focused pane's folder.
- Click a pane to focus it. While a tab has more than one pane, the focused one has an outline in
  the accent colour.
- Drag the line between two panes to resize them.
- Each pane keeps at least 20 columns and 5 rows. A split that would leave less is refused with a
  beep.
- The tab's title, its folder (where a new tab starts) and the find bar follow the focused pane.
- <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> on Linux) closes the focused
  pane and ends its shell; on the tab's last pane it closes the tab. The tab's × and **Close** still
  close the whole tab, with every pane in it.
- When a program other than the shell runs in what you close (an editor, an agent, a build), AIDE
  asks first: **Close this pane?** or **Close this tab?**, naming the programs. **Close** ends them,
  **Cancel** keeps them. A shell at its prompt closes at once.

#### Move between panes, resize and zoom

From the keyboard, the **Go** menu or the command palette:

| Action | Mac | Linux |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus the pane to the left / below / above / to the right** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>H</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd>, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd> and the arrows |
| **Move the divider left / down / up / right** | <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd> and the arrows | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd> and the arrows |
| **Zoom pane** | <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Z</kbd> |
| **Last pane** | none | none |
| **Show pane numbers** | none | none |

- **Focus** goes to the pane next to the focused one on that side, the one that lines up with it
  most. When two line up alike, the one you were in last wins. At the edge of the tab nothing
  happens.
- **Last pane** goes back to the pane you were in before.
- **Move the divider** takes the divider nearest the focused pane that runs across that direction
  (a vertical one for left and right, a horizontal one for up and down) and moves it that way, two
  columns (or two rows) a press. A pane never gets smaller than 20 columns and 5 rows.
- **Zoom pane** shows the focused pane alone, filling the tab, and its program gets the bigger size;
  the tab says **Zoomed**. Press it again to see every pane. Splitting, closing a pane or moving to
  another pane ends the zoom. A zoom isn't kept when AIDE or your computer restarts.
- **Show pane numbers** puts a number on each pane for two seconds; type one to move to that pane.
  <kbd>Esc</kbd> hides the numbers, and any other key hides them and goes to the pane.
- On the Mac, <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd> and <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd> with an arrow and <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd>
  are these pane keys now, so in a terminal they no longer move along the line or scroll. <kbd>⌘</kbd>
  with an arrow alone still does.
- These need the terminal server from the same release. An older one says **The terminal server is
  too old for this. Restart it to update it.**

#### Arrange a tab and type into every pane

Snap a tab's panes into a shape, move them around, or take one out into a tab of its own.
Right-click the tab in the status bar: **Layout** has the shapes and moves, then **Sync panes** and
**Break pane into a tab**. Each acts on that tab and shows it. The **Go** menu and the command palette have every one, and so
does the [prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key).

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Even horizontal layout** | Every pane side by side, equal widths |
| **Even vertical layout** | Every pane stacked, equal heights |
| **Main horizontal layout** | The first pane across the top (60%), the rest side by side beneath it |
| **Main vertical layout** | The first pane on the left (60%), the rest stacked to its right |
| **Tiled layout** | As close to a square grid as the panes allow: four are equal quarters |
| **Next layout** | The shape after the one this tab took last, in the order above |
| **Spread panes evenly** | Keeps the shape and evens out every divider: split, split again and split again gives 50%, 25%, 12.5%… until you spread them |
| **Swap with the previous pane** / **Swap with the next pane** | The focused pane trades places with the one before or after it; the focus stays on it |
| **Rotate panes** / **Rotate panes back** | Every pane moves one place along, the last one going round |
| **Break pane into a tab** | The focused pane leaves for a new tab right after this one, with its shell and what it shows |
| **Sync panes** (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>I</kbd> on the Mac) | What you type into one pane of the tab goes to all of them, until you turn it off |

- The panes keep their order: swap the one you want into the first place before a main layout to
  make it the big one.
- A shape or a move ends a zoom. The panes' programs keep running; each gets its new size.
- While a tab is synced, its status bar shows `SYNC`. Keys and pastes are copied; mouse clicks and
  scrolling aren't. A pane that isn't reading its input (its program is stuck) is skipped rather
  than holding the others up, and the flag says so: `SYNC · 1 skipped`. A paste reaches each pane
  as a paste, bracketed where that program asked for it.
- On the iPhone you see one pane at a time, so there is no layout there. What you type there into a
  synced tab's pane goes to its other panes all the same.
- Agents can do the same with [`aidectl terminal`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal): `layout`,
  `swap`, `rotate`, `break`, `sync` and `broadcast`, which types one text into many panes.
- These need the terminal server from the same release.

#### Name a pane

Give a pane a name to tell it from the others: choose **Rename pane…** from the **Go** menu or
the command palette, or press the [prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key) and then <kbd>T</kbd>, type the name
and choose **Rename**.

- In a tab of two or more panes, a named pane shows its name at its top right corner, the focused
  one's in the accent colour. A pane alone in its tab, or zoomed, shows none.
- The tab takes its focused pane's name instead of the program's title, unless you named the tab
  itself.
- The name stays when AIDE or your computer restarts. Rename it with an empty name to show the
  program's title again.
- On the iPhone the **Panes** list of an AIDE session names each pane by its name.
- Agents name panes with [`aidectl terminal rename-pane`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal), or give a pane its name as
  they start it (`split --name`, `spawn --name`).

#### Copy mode

Copy mode moves through a pane's text and its scrollback with vi's
keys, so you can select and copy without the mouse. Press <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>Y</kbd> on the
Mac or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Y</kbd> on Linux (**Copy mode** in the **Go** menu on the Mac
and in the command palette), or the [prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key) and then
<kbd>[</kbd>. A cursor appears where the terminal's cursor was, the status bar shows `COPY`, and the
bottom of the pane says **Copy mode**. The keys are comux's:

| Keys | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>h</kbd> <kbd>j</kbd> <kbd>k</kbd> <kbd>l</kbd>, or the arrows | Move |
| <kbd>w</kbd> <kbd>b</kbd> <kbd>e</kbd> | Next word, previous word, end of the word (stopping at punctuation) |
| <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd> | The same by words between blanks |
| <kbd>0</kbd> <kbd>^</kbd> <kbd>$</kbd> | Start of the line, its first character, its end |
| <kbd>H</kbd> <kbd>M</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd> | Top, middle and bottom of the view |
| <kbd>&#123;</kbd> <kbd>&#125;</kbd> | Previous and next blank line |
| <kbd>%</kbd> | The bracket that matches the first one from the cursor |
| <kbd>f</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> <kbd>t</kbd> <kbd>T</kbd> and a character, then <kbd>;</kbd> <kbd>,</kbd> | To that character on the line (or just before it), again, the other way |
| <kbd>g</kbd> | The top of the scrollback |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>U</kbd> <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>D</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>B</kbd> <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>F</kbd>, <kbd>Page Up</kbd> <kbd>Page Down</kbd> | Half a page, a page |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Y</kbd> <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>E</kbd>, <kbd>K</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> | Scroll a line |
| <kbd>/</kbd> <kbd>?</kbd>, then <kbd>n</kbd> <kbd>N</kbd> | Search down or up, then the next match or the one before |
| <kbd>*</kbd> <kbd>#</kbd> | Search for the word under the cursor, down or up |
| <kbd>v</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> | Select characters |
| <kbd>V</kbd> | Select lines |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>V</kbd> | Select a rectangle |
| <kbd>o</kbd> | Go to the other end of the selection |
| <kbd>y</kbd> or <kbd>Return</kbd> | Copy and leave |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Clear the selection, or leave |
| <kbd>q</kbd>, <kbd>G</kbd> or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>C</kbd> | Leave |

- <kbd>y</kbd> copies the selection to the clipboard, or the cursor's whole line when nothing is
  selected, and says how many lines it copied. A line the terminal wrapped because it was too long
  copies as one line.
- Pressing <kbd>v</kbd>, <kbd>V</kbd> or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>V</kbd> while another kind of selection
  is up changes its shape and keeps where it started; the same key again clears it.
- Search ignores case and goes round at the ends. Every match in view lights up as you type, and
  stays lit after <kbd>Return</kbd>. You can type the search with an input method, Korean included.
- The wheel scrolls and a click puts the cursor on the character you clicked.
- Copy mode reads the pane when you press the key. The program keeps running; when its output
  moves the rows, the pane shows the text you're copying from, without its colours, until you
  leave.
- **Copy mode · the text may be inexact** means AIDE couldn't tell where the terminal wrapped
  lines: each row then copies as a line of its own.
- Making the pane wider or narrower, or changing the text size, ends copy mode. A change in its
  height (a bar above it appearing, say) keeps copy mode and shows more or fewer rows. Leaving takes
  the view back to the bottom.
- The iPhone has no copy mode: touch and hold to select text as before.

### Beside your notes

The right panel (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd> on
Linux) has a **Terminal** view: the same tabs and the same shells, next to whatever the main area
shows, such as a note. While the main area is the Terminal section, the shells are there, so the
panel doesn't offer its Terminal view. If the panel was showing it, it
shows **Outline** there instead; the panel keeps Terminal as its view, so it's back when you go to
another section.

#### Work graph

The right panel's **Work graph** view shows what each Claude Code or
Codex agent in your panes has read, edited, written, searched and run, as a graph that grows while
it works. Open it from the panel's views, or choose **Work graph** in the command palette.

**Settings › Terminal › Layout › Right panel views** (on Linux also
`right` under `[terminal.layout]` in `config.toml`) lists the right panel's configurable views in
their order: `agents`, `notices`, `today` and `workgraph`, all four by default. Type the names
separated by commas and press <kbd>Return</kbd>; a name AIDE doesn't know is dropped and said under
the field. Empty leaves them all out, and the Work graph out of the command palette too.

- **This agent** shows one agent (pick it in the menu beside); **All agents** shows every agent, with
  the files more than one of them touched marked.
- Each file is a dot, bigger the more it was touched; hover for how often it was read, edited,
  written and searched. **Filter files** narrows them by name.
- The strip at the bottom is the agent's steps in order: drag along it to go back, **▶** replays from
  the start, **Live** follows the newest step. The line beside it adds them up, such as
  `3 files · 2 reads · 1 edit · 1 run`.
- **Go to its pane** (or a click on the agent) brings its pane forward, in its space if another one is
  on screen. Double-click a file to open it: a note of your vault opens in AIDE, any other file in an
  editor split beside that agent's pane, as a path printed there does.
- Only what succeeded counts. A shell command counts only for what its exit status proves (`cat
  notes.md` read `notes.md`); anything else shows as a run.
- AIDE reads the agents' own conversation files on the computer the terminal server runs on, and
  keeps each graph there for 30 days, so it's still there after AIDE or the server restarts. Turn
  it off with **Keep a work graph** in [Settings › Terminal › Agents](#settings--terminal--agents):
  an open panel then says the work graph is off, and fills again as soon as you turn it back on.
- On the iPhone, **Work graph** at the top of a host's **Agents** sheet shows that host's graph; tap
  an agent to open its pane. Files there are the host's, so a double-click shows the path instead.
- `aidectl terminal graph` prints the same (see [aidectl](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal)).

#### Agents in the right panel

Beside the Terminal section, the right panel's **Agents** view lists your
agents as the [agent overview](#agent-overview) does, and shows one of them in detail above the list:
the agent in the pane you're in, or the one you click, until you go to another pane. In a pane with
no agent it goes on showing the one it showed. Other sections don't offer it, nor does a `right` that leaves
`agents` out; the panel remembers it for the Terminal section and shows its own view elsewhere.

- Its name and state, what it asks or is doing, and how full its context is, such as
  **Context 62%** (in the warning colour from 80%).
- The [card](/docs/guides/agent-board/) its pane works on, with how many of its steps are done and each
  step with its mark.
- **Recent tool calls**: its last five, newest first, each with what it did (**Read**, **Edit**,
  **Write**, **Delete**, **Search**, **Run**, **Web** or **Subagent**), what it was about, such as the
  file it touched, the command it ran, what it searched for or the subagent's task, and **Running** or
  **Failed** when it's either. They come from the
  [work graph](#work-graph) and update every few seconds while the view shows; with **Keep a work
  graph** off it says **None yet**.

**Focused** and **All** at the top of the view switch between that and a
board of every agent. The choice is per window and isn't kept. **All** shows:

- How many agents are **Working**, **Needs you** (waiting for your answer), **Unseen** (finished
  while you weren't looking) and **Idle**, the two that want you highlighted while they have any.
- The agents in groups: **Needs you** (the longest waiting first), **Unseen** (the newest first) and
  **Working** (by how full their context is, **by context**), each with its space and tab, what it's
  doing and its context. Up to seven show per group, then **N more**; a click goes to its pane.
- **Idle** with its count and the two idle longest (**Longest: …**), without rows.
- Your Claude and Codex usage limits at the bottom, as the status bar reads them.

#### Notifications in the right panel

The right panel's **Notifications** view is the
[notification center](#notification-center) in the panel, in every section while the terminal is
on (unless `right` above leaves `notices` out): the same rows, **Clear all** and **Dismiss**, and <kbd>↑</kbd>, <kbd>↓</kbd>, <kbd>Return</kbd> and
<kbd>Delete</kbd> as there. Going to a pane from it leaves the panel as it is. A terminal server from an older AIDE says to restart it for the notification center.

Find goes on past the last match to the first, and says **No matches**
when there are none, on the Mac and Linux.

For the tab, pane, find and text-size keys, see [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#terminal).

### The status bar

On the Mac and Linux the Terminal section has one status bar under the panes, as comux and tmux have. The
tab row at the top is gone: everything you used it for is in the bar. From left to right:

- **The space pill**: the [space](#spaces) on screen and its git branch. Click it to switch spaces,
  or for **New space…**, **Rename space…** and **End space…**. A needs-you mark before it means a
  coding agent in another space is waiting for you.
- **The tabs**, as `1:zsh`, `2:claude`: the number and the name. The tab on screen is highlighted.
  A tab with a coding agent shows what it is doing; `Z` means the tab has a pane zoomed, and `!` that
  a program rang the bell there since you last looked. A tab on another computer starts with its
  host, as `box:3:zsh`. Click a tab to show it, double-click to rename it, right-click for its menu;
  its × shows on the tab on screen and when you point at another. **+** opens a new tab.
- On the right: `ZOOM` while the tab on screen has a pane zoomed, `SYNC` while it's
  [synced](#arrange-a-tab-and-type-into-every-pane) , `COPY` while its pane is in
  [copy mode](#copy-mode), `LOCK` while the
  [switcher key](#the-switcher-key) is locked (click it to unlock), `^B` while the
  [prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key) waits for its next key, then the agents in every space,
  each count after its mark from the Agents list (working, needs you, and done: finished, and you
  haven't looked yet), the usage readout, and the clock. Click the needs-you count to
  [jump to the agent that needs you](#jump-to-an-agent), the done count to jump to the one that
  finished last, and the working count to open the [agent overview](#agent-overview).
- After the flags, a mic: click it to
  [say a prompt](/docs/guides/voice-prompts/) to the agent in the pane you're in, and again to stop
  (<kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>M</kbd> on the Mac, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>M</kbd> on
  Linux). It's orange while it listens, and greyed out with **No agent in this pane** when there's no
  agent to talk to. A status bar you arranged yourself gets it only when you add `voice`.
- Before the agent counts, a bell and the number of unread
  [notifications](#notification-center) of panes still open. Click it to go to the newest one's
  pane (another pane's first, when the one you're in has some too), its space first; the
  notification is read once the pane shows. A pane closed since says **That pane is gone**.
- While the Terminal section's panel shows its list (Agents or Tabs), the list also offers what the
  space menu does: right-click a space's heading, or its row under **Spaces**, for **Open space**,
  **Show todos** (for a space with a project, while the board is on), **Rename space…**,
  **New worktree…**, **End space…**, **New space…** and **New space from layout…**.
  The bar keeps everything then too: the pill (or the chips) and the
  agent counts stay on it.

Each tab is a rounded box: its number in grey, its agent's mark, then
its name, with the tab on screen filled in the accent colour. A tab you haven't named is called by
the title its program set (an agent's task, `vim notes.md`), or by the program itself (`zsh`) when
the title is only the shell's prompt (`you@host: ~/dir`). Its × shows only while you point at it.

Press <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>9</kbd> on the Mac, or
<kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>9</kbd> on Linux, to show a tab by its number;
<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>9</kbd> still work on the Mac. A program that wants
Option with a digit for itself (readline, emacs) gets it back with **Settings › Terminal ›
Option+digit switches tabs** off (on Linux, **Alt+digit switches
tabs**), and you can give the tabs other keys in **Settings › Keyboard**.

When the window is narrow, only the tabs scroll: every tab stays whole,
the one on screen is kept in view, and a trackpad or the mouse wheel over them scrolls them sideways.
While some tabs are out of view, a **+N** button after them carries the mark of the most pressing
agent among those (▲ needs you, then ✓ done, then ● working), so a blocked tab out of sight still
shows; click it for a menu of every tab, and choose one to go to it. **+** for a new tab stays in
view too. Everything on the right stays whole. Only when even that doesn't fit
beside a little room for the tabs does it give way, in this order: the reset times, the names in the
usage (`Claude` to `C`, then none), the percentages, the 5-hour limits, then the flags and the clock
together (the first flag, `LOCK` while it's on, and `+N` for the rest; the clock without its date or
seconds), and last the gauges, which become one icon that lists every limit when you point at it.

A tab whose agent reports how much of its context window it has used has a thin line under its label,
as long as that share: green, orange from 80%.

Claude Code names its context window only in its status line. Unless
[`aidectl agent install-hooks`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent) put AIDE's report in yours, AIDE
measures a Claude Code's context against 200k tokens, or 1M once it has used more than that, and
marks its share with `~`, such as `~62%`; another agent whose window isn't known shows its tokens,
such as `131k`. A one-line row in the left list shows how full its context is whenever that's
known.

The usage readout comes last: Claude's 5-hour and weekly limits, and Codex's. The terminal server
reads them for every AIDE on the computer, from Claude Code's own sign-in and Codex's newest session,
about once a minute while AIDE is open.

- AIDE reads the sign-in Claude Code itself uses: on the Mac the
  **Claude Code-credentials** item in the login Keychain (the first time, macOS may ask whether
  `security` can read it), and the credentials file only when there is no such item; on Linux the
  credentials file. A sign-in about to expire isn't used, and one the usage service refuses counts as
  expired: open Claude Code to renew it. AIDE never refreshes or changes the sign-in, and tries no
  other one. When it can't read one, the readout says why (the sign-in has expired, or the Keychain
  couldn't be read).

- Claude and Codex each get one row: the name, then each limit's
  window (`5h`, `wk`), a small bar as full as that limit is used, the percentage and how long until
  it resets (`⟳ 2h13m`). The bar turns yellow from 70% and red from 90%. Point at the usage for every
  limit in words.
- When nothing of Claude's could be read, its row is `Claude —`, and
  pointing at it says why (the sign-in has expired, the Keychain couldn't be read). Codex's row shows
  once a Codex session has given its limits.
- Right-click it for **Refresh** and **Status bar settings…**, and
  **Token and cost report…** ([below](#token-and-cost-report)).
- When a limit can't be read right now, the last reading stays, marked `~` (`~Claude`), for up to
  3 hours.

Change it in **Settings › Terminal › Status bar**:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Show status bar** | On | Off hides the right side of the bar; the pill, the tabs and **+** stay. |
| **Position** | **Under the panes** | **Over the panes** puts the bar where the tab row was. |
| **Spaces** | **The shown space** | **The shown space** is the pill; **Every space** shows a chip for each space instead, with a dot on one whose agent needs you. The chips stay in view, wherever the space is on the bar; when they don't all fit, the pill shows instead, and its menu lists every space. |
| **Tab labels** | **Number and name** | **Number and name** (`2 zsh`, the number in grey), **Name**, or **Number**. |
| **Clock** | On | Off hides the clock. |
| **Clock format** | `%H:%M` | `%H` `%M` `%S` (24-hour time), `%I` `%p` (12-hour), `%a` `%b` `%d` `%m` `%Y` `%y` (the date), `%%` for a percent sign. |

| **Resets** | **Time left** | **Time left** (`⟳ 2h13m`), **Clock time** (`⟳ 14:32`, or `⟳ Wed 09:00` a day or more ahead), or **Off**. |

On Linux the same settings are also in `config.toml` under `[terminal.status_bar]` (`show`,
`position` as `bottom` or `top`, `spaces` as `current` or `all`, `tab_labels` as `both`, `name` or
`index`, `clock`, `clock_format`, `usage`, `usage_reset`, `usage_windows`), with the names and values
comux used. AIDE writes only the settings you changed from their default, so a later default reaches
the rest. The old page settings (`usage_layout`, `usage_page_unit`, `usage_rotate_secs`,
`usage_bar_width`) are ignored when set.

#### Arrange the bar

You choose what the bar holds, in what order, how a tab is labelled
and what colour each part takes. The same settings, with the same names, work on the Mac and Linux,
and both read them the same way.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Tab format** | Empty | How a tab is labelled; empty uses **Tab labels**. See below. |
| **Left side** | `space, tabs` | The parts at the left end, in order. |
| **Right side** | `flags, voice, attention, agents, usage, clock` (`voice` new) | The parts at the right end, in order. |
| **Tab color**, **Active tab color**, **Mode flags color**, **Clock color** | **Default** | **Text**, **Secondary text**, **Accent**, **Success**, **Warning**, **Danger** or **Info**. |

The parts are `space` (the pill, or the chips with **Every space**), `tabs` (with **+** after them),
`flags` (`ZOOM`, `^B` and `LOCK`), `voice` (the mic), `attention` (the bell), `agents`, `usage` and `clock`. A right side saved as an old default, `flags, agents, usage, clock` or `flags, attention, agents, usage, clock` in that order, gets what came after it (`attention`, `voice`); any other keeps its parts until you add them. Each part goes in one side only, and the tabs
are always there: left out of both sides, they join the left. **Show status bar** off keeps only the
space and the tabs, where you put them, and **Clock** off drops the clock; your order stays for when
you turn them back on. A name AIDE doesn't know shows under the field and is left out.

A tab format is text with names in braces:

- `{index}` is the tab's number, `{name}` its name, `{host}` the computer of a remote tab (empty for
  a local one), `{active}` whether it's the tab on screen, and `{agent}` what its coding agent is
  doing: `working`, `blocked` (needs you), `done`, `waiting`, `idle`, or empty.
- `{if active}…{end}` shows its part only for the tab on screen; `{if not active}`, `{elif …}` and
  `{else}` work too, and so do `{if agent == "blocked"}` and `{if index != 1}`.
- `{style danger}…{end}` colours its part, with the colour names above (`text`, `text-secondary`,
  `accent`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `info`).
- `{{` and `}}` are a brace.

For example, `{index} {name}{if active} ●{end}` labels the tab on screen `2 zsh ●`, and
`{if agent == "blocked"}{style danger}!{end} {end}{index}:{name}` puts a red `!` before a tab whose
agent needs you. The bar still draws a remote tab's computer, the agent's mark, `Z` and `!` itself.
A label is at most 256 characters, the last one `…` when it's cut.

A format AIDE can't read shows under the field with where the problem is, such as
`Line 1, column 10: Unknown name “nam” (one of index, name, host, active, agent)`, and Return selects
it. The format is kept as you wrote it, so you can fix it, and the tabs use **Tab labels** until it
reads.

On Linux they're `tab_format`, `left`, `right` and `style` in `[terminal.status_bar]`:

```toml
[terminal.status_bar]
left = ["tabs"]
right = ["agents", "clock", "space"]
tab_format = "{index}{if active}*{end}"
style.tab_active = "accent"
style.clock = "text-secondary"
```

An agent can read the bar's settings and check a change before you make it:
[`aidectl status-bar`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#status-bar).

To stop reading one of them at all, set `usage` in the terminal server's `config.json`
(`~/Library/Application Support/AIDE/aide-mux/config.json` on the Mac,
`~/.local/state/aide-mux/config.json` on Linux), for example `"usage": {"codex": false}`. Nothing is
then read for it, and the bar doesn't show it.

On the iPhone, a host's **AIDE on** screen shows the same limits under its title.
They're the bar's rows: Claude's and Codex's, each limit with its small
bar, percentage and reset, and `Claude —` before anything is read. When they don't fit on one line,
each provider takes a line; with larger text, or on a narrow screen, a provider's limits take one line
each, and a reset moves under its bar when even that doesn't fit. When Claude shows `—`, the reason
shows under the rows. Tap them for that host's
[token and cost report](#token-and-cost-report).

An agent can read them too, before it starts a big task: `aidectl agent usage` (see
[aidectl](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent)).

#### Token and cost report

**Token and cost report…** shows how many tokens Claude Code and Codex
used on this computer and what that would cost at the providers' prices, model by model. Right-click
the usage readout and choose it, or choose **Token and cost report…** in the command palette. AIDE
reads it from the agents' own conversation files on the computer the terminal server runs on.

- Pick **Today**, **7 days** or **30 days** (by your own calendar: today's midnight, then 6 or 29
  days before).
- Each model has a row with its input, cache writes, cache reads, output, total and cost, and the
  last row adds them up. A message Claude Code wrote several times counts once.
- Prices come from a table dated in the report's notes. A model the table doesn't know shows `?`:
  its tokens count, its cost doesn't, and a total that leaves it out ends in `+`. Codex's costs are
  estimates, marked `~`, since a ChatGPT plan isn't billed by the token.
- **Refresh** reads the files again; the report is otherwise kept for 30 seconds.
- **Save as note** adds the report as a section, **Token and cost report, 14:32**, at the end of
  today's daily note (made from your template if it isn't there yet).
- On the iPhone, tap the usage line on a host's **AIDE on** screen to see that host's report. It
  saves to the iPhone's own notes.
- `aidectl terminal usage` prints the same table (see
  [aidectl](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal)), and `aide-mux usage` prints it without AIDE running,
  as `comux usage` did.

### Spaces



A space is a set of tabs of its own, started in a folder you pick, such as one per project. Each
window shows one space at a time and switches between them; the shells of the others keep running.

- Your vault's own tabs are its first space, named after the vault.
- **Spaces** at the top of the section's panel lists them, each with its git branch and how many
  tabs it has. Click one to show it.
- The menu at the start of the tab row (the pill in [the status bar](#the-status-bar))
  shows the space you're in and its branch. Open it to switch,
  or for **New space…**, **Rename space…** and **End space…**.
- Every window on the computer lists the same spaces, and each window remembers the one it showed
  for its vault the next time it opens.

To make one: <Badge text="Next release" />

1. Press <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>S</kbd> on Linux),
   or choose **New space…**. A search field opens, ready for typing, over a list of folders: the
   folder of the tab you're in, folders you made spaces in lately, your spaces' folders, and the git
   repositories (with their worktrees) in `~/dev`, `~/src`, `~/code`, `~/projects`, `~/repos`, `~/git`,
   `~/work`, `~/workspace` and `~/Developer`, up to two folders deep.
2. Type a few letters of the folder. They don't have to be next to each other: `aide` and `dvai` both
   find `~/dev/aide`. The letters you typed show in bold, folders you use often come first, and the
   line under the field says what <kbd>Return</kbd> does. Or type a path starting with `/` or `~/` to
   list that folder's folders (start the last part with `.` to see hidden ones); <kbd>Tab</kbd> fills
   in the highlighted folder's path, so you can keep going into it.
3. Press <kbd>Return</kbd>. The space is made in that folder, named after it. When the folder already
   has a space, its row says **Open space** and the space's name, and <kbd>Return</kbd> shows that
   space instead of making a second one. When New space… is opened for a project, only that project's space counts, so a folder whose space belongs elsewhere gets a new one.

<kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> move (<kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>P</kbd> and <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> too, or
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>P</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> on Linux). To give the space another name,
or to make a second space in a folder that has one, press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd>
(<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Enter</kbd> on Linux), type the name and press <kbd>Return</kbd>; <kbd>Esc</kbd>
goes back to the list, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> in the list closes it. A folder's name that another space
already has gets a number: `aide 2`.

The new space opens with a shell in its folder, and its new tabs start there too. Splits start in the
folder of the pane you split.

| Action | Mac | Linux |
|---|---|---|
| **New space…** | <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>S</kbd> |
| **Next space** | <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> |
| **Previous space** | <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> |

- The keys work while a terminal pane has the keyboard. **Rename space…** and **End space…** have no
  key; give them one in **Settings › Keyboard**.
- **End space…** asks first and names the programs still running there; its shells end. Ending the
  space you're in shows your vault's space again. If another window or computer ends the space on
  screen, the window goes back to your vault's space and says **The space … was ended.**
- With **Keep shells running after AIDE quits** off and **Reopen tabs at launch** on, quitting ends
  the spaces' shells but keeps their tabs. The spaces stay in the list as **Not running**, AIDE opens
  on the space you were in, and showing one starts its shells again in its folders.
- The branch is read from the folder's git files; it follows a `git checkout` within a few seconds.

#### Build a space from a layout file

A layout file describes a whole space in one TOML file: its folder, its
tabs, each tab's layout and what each pane runs. Choose **New space from layout…** in the space menu
(also on a space's right-click menu and in the command palette), pick the file, and AIDE makes the
space and shows it. It reads the same files as comux's `apply-layout`.

```toml
name = "api"            # the space's name (default: the file's name)
cwd = "~/dev/api"       # where it starts (default: the file's folder)

[[tab]]
name = "code"
layout = "main-vertical"  # even-horizontal, even-vertical, main-horizontal, main-vertical or tiled (the default)

[[tab.pane]]              # the first pane is the main one
run = "nvim ."

[[tab.pane]]
run = ["cargo build", "cargo watch -x test"]   # several lines, in order

[[tab.pane]]              # a plain shell

[[tab]]
name = "agents"

[[tab.pane]]
cwd = "web"               # relative to the folder above it
run = "claude"
```

- Every pane is a shell. Its `run` lines are typed into it, one <kbd>Return</kbd> each, once every
  pane has started, so your aliases and shell settings apply and the lines are in the shell's
  history. A program that reads its input can read the lines meant for after it.
- A folder can be absolute, start with `~`, or be relative to the one above it (the file's folder,
  then the space's, then the tab's).
- Nothing is made when the file names a folder that isn't there, or has a mistake: AIDE says which
  folder, or where the mistake is (`tab 2 pane 1: …`). An unknown key counts as a mistake, so a typo
  isn't quietly a plain shell.
- A file holds at most 32 tabs, 16 panes in a tab and 64 in all, and is at most 16 KiB. A `run` line
  is one line of at most 1000 bytes.
- Agents and scripts can use [`aidectl terminal layout apply`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal),
  whose `--dry-run` prints what it would make without making it.
- This needs the terminal server from the same release.

#### Restart an ended shell

With **Close a tab when its shell exits** off in **Settings › Terminal**,
a pane whose shell ends stays with what it showed. Right-click its tab in the status bar and choose
**Restart shell** (also in the **Go** menu and the command palette): a new shell starts in the same
pane, in the folder the old one was last in, below what was there. On the iPhone, **Restart shell**
is in the **Session** menu.

A program an agent started with [`aidectl terminal spawn --keep`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal)
needs none of this: when it ends, its pane says `[exited N]` (its exit code) and goes on as a shell.

#### Switch, go back, mark

Get back to where you just were, to a pane you marked, or to any tab by a few letters of its name
(comux's <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>f</kbd>, `;` and marks):

- **Switch space, tab or agent…** (<kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>O</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>O</kbd>
  on Linux) opens a list of your spaces, agents and tabs. Type to narrow it the way **Open a note…**
  does; <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> switch between **Spaces**, **Agents** and **Tabs** (turn on
  **All spaces** for every space's tabs), <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> move, <kbd>Return</kbd> goes
  there and <kbd>F2</kbd> renames a space. Under the list, the selected one's last lines show.
- **Spaces**, **Agents** and **Tabs** each show how many they have, or
  how many match what you typed. On **Agents**, with nothing typed, the agents come in groups: **Needs
  you** (waiting for your answer, then finished while you weren't looking), **Working**, then
  **Idle**, each the most recently changed first; <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> pass over the
  headings. A row has the state's mark (● working, ✓ done, ▲ needs you, ○ waiting or idle), its title,
  its space and tab, what it's at (**Not updating** while AIDE can't confirm its state, what it waits
  for while it needs you, otherwise what it's doing) and, from 80%, how full its context is. Typing
  ranks them all in one list, matching what the rows show as well as the state and how full the
  context is. With an agent selected, the preview says how full its context is when the agent reports
  it (when the size of its window isn't known, a token count such as `131k`, or
  for Claude Code a share of an estimated window marked `~`) and how
  long it has been in its state, and shows its card under its last lines when its pane works on one.
- **List tabs…** opens the same list on **Tabs** with the tab you were on before selected, so it
  and then <kbd>Return</kbd> flip between two tabs.
- **Back to the previous place**, **Forward to the next place** and **Last place** walk the places
  this window has been, across spaces and tabs, like a browser's history. **Last place** goes back
  and forth between the last two.
- **Mark this pane…** asks for a digit, 1 to 9, and keeps the pane under it; **Go to a mark…**
  lists your marks and shows the one whose digit you press, switching space if it has to. Marks are
  your computer's, so every window and `aidectl terminal mark` share them, and they come back
  after a restart. A space that ended keeps its marks for when you show it again.
- **Move tab left** and **Move tab right** move the tab you're on along the bar
  (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Page Up</kbd> and <kbd>Page Down</kbd> on Linux).

Only the switcher, the [switcher key](#the-switcher-key) and Linux's move-tab keys have a
shortcut; give the others one in **Settings › Keyboard**, or run them from
the command palette. Marks and the switcher's preview
need the terminal server from this version: an older one shows **The terminal server needs
updating** until it restarts.

#### The switcher key

One key reaches the switcher from any pane, as comux's <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>f</kbd> does. While a pane
has the keyboard, press <kbd>⌃</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> on the Mac or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> on Linux:
the switcher opens, and the program in the pane doesn't get the key. Press it again, or
<kbd>Esc</kbd>, to close the switcher; the keyboard is back in the pane you opened it from.

- It acts only in a pane. In a note, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> on Linux is still **Find in note**.
  On the Mac, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> is still **Find in note**, and the terminal's find bar in a
  pane.
- It's the action **Open the switcher from a pane**. Give it another shortcut in **Settings ›
  Keyboard**, or **Clear** it there, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> goes to the program again (see
  [In a terminal](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#in-a-terminal)).
- While an input method is composing (Korean, Japanese, Chinese), or the pane is in
  [copy mode](#copy-mode), the key goes to the pane.

For a program that wants <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> itself (readline's forward-char, Emacs), lock
the switcher key: press the [prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key), then <kbd>g</kbd>
(**Lock the switcher key**). While it's locked, the key goes to the program and the
[status bar](#the-status-bar) shows `LOCK`. Press the prefix and <kbd>g</kbd> again, or click
`LOCK`, to unlock. The lock is the window's own, and it holds only the switcher key: the prefix,
<kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>O</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>O</kbd>) and your other
shortcuts keep working. **Lock the switcher key** has no shortcut of its own until you give it one
in **Settings › Keyboard**.

The switcher key isn't on the iPhone.

#### Spaces and projects

A space made for a project (`aidectl terminal space new --project`, or **Open space** below) joins
that project's todos:

- The space menu, and a space's menu in **Spaces**, have **Show todos**: **Todos › Board** opens on
  that project's lane alone. It's there while the agent board is on.
- On the board, right-click a project's filter chip for **Open space**. A project's note (one with
  `type: project`, or in a `projects/` folder) has **Open this project's space** in the command
  palette. Either shows the newest space with that project, or opens **New space…** with the project
  filled in.

#### Worktrees

A worktree is a second checkout of your repository on a branch of its own, so an agent can work on
one branch while you stay on another (comux's worktrees). AIDE makes one with its own space:

1. In the repository's space, choose **New worktree…** from the space menu, a space's menu in
   **Spaces**, or the command palette.
2. **Repository** is the git checkout of the pane you're in. Type the new **Branch** and, if it
   shouldn't start where your checkout is now, **Start from** (a branch, tag or commit).
3. Choose **Create**. The worktree goes in a folder beside the repository, `<repository>-<branch>`
   (`/` in the branch becomes `-`), and its space, named after the branch, opens there.

- A worktree's space has the branch icon in **Spaces** and in the space pill.
- **Settings › Terminal › Worktrees** can give a repository a command its new worktrees run first,
  such as `pnpm install`: it runs in the new space's first pane, where you can watch it or press
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>C</kbd>, and then the pane is your shell. The command gets the worktree's
  folder as `$WORKTREE_PATH` (and `$WORKTREE_BRANCH`, `$WORKTREE_MAIN`). **Folder name** changes how
  the folder is named, from `{repo}` and `{branch}`.
- **End space…** on a worktree's space also offers **Also remove the worktree** and **Delete the
  branch**. Nothing is removed while a pane of another space is in its folder, while it is your
  repository's main checkout or it is locked. Uncommitted or untracked files are asked about first:
  **Discard changes and remove**, or **Keep it**. The branch is deleted only when it has been merged;
  otherwise the worktree goes and AIDE says the branch stayed, with git's reason.
- From an [agent card](/docs/guides/agent-board/), **Start in a worktree…** does the same for the
  card (see [Cards linked to panes](#cards-linked-to-panes)).
- `aidectl terminal worktree create`, `list` and `rm` do it from a shell (see
  [aidectl](/docs/developers/aidectl/)).

Worktrees need the terminal server from this version: an older one shows **The terminal server needs
updating** until it restarts. The iPhone has no **New worktree…**; a worktree's space made on a
desktop shows in its list like any other.

### Shells that outlive AIDE

Your shells run in AIDE's terminal server, a small program of its own, not in the app. Quitting
AIDE, closing its window or AIDE closing unexpectedly leaves them running, with whatever runs in
them: a build, an SSH session, an agent. When you open AIDE again, each tab and pane is back as you
left it, still running, with its screen and scrollback.

- On the Mac the terminal server comes inside the app and runs as a login item (it can show under
  **System Settings › General › Login Items**). On Linux it's installed beside the app and runs as
  the `aide-mux.service` systemd user service when your session has a user manager; otherwise AIDE
  starts it on its own.
- Each vault has its own set of tabs on the server. On the Mac, two windows of the same vault show
  the same tabs.
- The first time you quit with shells open, AIDE says once that **Your shells keep running**, and
  where to turn that off.
- On the Mac, while AIDE reaches the server, the Terminal section says **Starting the terminal…**.
  If the server can't be reached, it says **The terminal server isn't running**, with the reason and
  **Try again**.
- A server left from an older (or newer) AIDE that this AIDE can't talk to says so: **The terminal
  server is from an older AIDE**, with **Restart it**. Restarting asks first
  (**Restart the terminal server?**), since its shells and the programs in them end.
- After AIDE updates itself, it updates the terminal server that was
  running in place, the next time it connects to it. Your shells and the programs in them, such as an
  agent mid-turn or a build, keep running in the same process with the same screen and scrollback:
  their output pauses for a moment, usually well under a second, and nothing restarts. Nothing is
  shown when that works.
- If the update doesn't take, the old server keeps running with your shells, and a bar over the tabs
  says **This version's terminal server couldn't take over** with the reason, and that the old
  version keeps running, with **Try again** and a close that puts the bar away until you quit AIDE.
- A terminal server from before servers could update in place says **This terminal server can't
  update in place; restarting it ends its shells.**, with **Restart it**, which asks first as above.
  Nothing restarts by itself.
- A server that this AIDE can't talk to at all is asked to update in place first. Only if that
  doesn't work does the Terminal section offer **Restart it**, and says why the update didn't work.
- On Linux, if the update couldn't put the new terminal server in place, the bar says **This
  version's terminal server couldn't be installed** with the reason, and offers nothing. While the
  update is still being installed, it says so, and the server is updated once it's done.
- Quitting AIDE to install an update never ends your shells, even with **Keep shells running after
  AIDE quits** turned off, and doesn't ask about running programs.
- On the iPhone, a host's AIDE sessions keep going while that host's
  terminal server updates: a pane pauses for a moment and goes on, with no reconnect.
- If the connection to the terminal server is lost anyway, your tabs,
  splits and panes stay on screen while AIDE connects again. When it reaches the same server, each
  pane picks up where it is, with the same tab on screen and its program still running; a pane
  whose shell ended in the meantime closes as usual. Only when the server is a new one (the old one
  stopped) are the tabs made again from it. On the iPhone, AIDE connects again by itself once
  before it says **Disconnected from** the host with **Reconnect**.
- With no AIDE window attached, the server keeps running as long as it has shells. It stops by
  itself only after 7 days with no window and no shell running.

To have quitting end your shells instead, turn off **Settings › Terminal › Keep shells running
after AIDE quits**. Then:

- Quitting AIDE asks first when a program other than the shell is running in a pane: **End the
  running programs?** lists them, with **Quit** and **Cancel**. On the Mac, closing the last window
  of a vault asks the same, with **Close**; on Linux, closing the window asks.
- After you quit, the server ends the shells of the vaults this AIDE showed, unless another AIDE
  (on another computer, say) still has them open. The layout is kept, and the next start opens it
  with new shells.
- A server with no window for 24 hours ends its shells and stops.

### Tabs after a restart

The terminal server keeps each vault's layout: its tabs with their names, the splits at their sizes,
the tab you were on, and the folder each pane's shell is in. When the server starts again, after
your computer restarts say, the layout comes back with a new shell in each pane, in its folder.
The scrollback and the environment don't come back; a folder that's gone opens at home instead.

Some programs come back too, as tmux-resurrect brings them back:

- **Claude Code and Codex** start again in the conversation they had, with the options that still
  apply: `claude --resume <conversation> --dangerously-skip-permissions`, say, or
  `codex resume <conversation>`. What you first typed after `claude` isn't sent again. An agent
  whose conversation AIDE doesn't know, one run with `-p` or a one-shot subcommand such as `codex exec`, and every agent while
  **Bring back agents in their conversation** is off, comes back as a shell.
- **Editors and other full-screen programs** on **Programs to bring back** run again as they were
  started, with their files: Neovim, Vim, Emacs in the terminal, nano, Helix, less, man, htop,
  btop, top, lazygit, tig, yazi, ranger and nnn by default. The agents (Claude Code, Codex and the
  others AIDE knows) are on the list too; take a name off to bring that program back as a shell.
- **Anything else**, such as a server, a watcher or a REPL, comes back as a shell in its folder, so
  nothing with side effects starts by itself. Add its name to the list if you want it run again.

AIDE notices these programs while **Watch for coding agents** is on. Each program runs in its
pane's shell, so quitting it leaves you at a prompt. Only what the program
had in front of the pane is kept, and only when its keyboard and screen were the pane's: a
program at the end of a pipe (`git log | less`) or writing into one comes back as a shell. What
the program held itself, such as unsaved changes, undo history, its splits or where you were in a
file, isn't kept; an editor may say it found a swap file.

To do this, AIDE keeps each of these panes' command lines on your computer, readable only by you,
beside the saved layout. A password or token you typed on such a command line is kept with it; take
the program off the list, or turn **Reopen tabs at launch** off, if that matters.

- The layout is saved a second after each change, a `cd` included.
- A tab you closed stays closed.
- When you close every tab, the next start opens one fresh shell.
- Turning **Reopen tabs at launch** off forgets the saved layouts.
- The first time the new AIDE starts, the tabs an earlier AIDE had saved are taken over.

### Reopen a past conversation

Claude Code, Codex and Grok keep every conversation on your computer, so one you closed days ago can
be opened again, or forked into a new one, as comux's resume picker does.

1. In a terminal, press <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>R</kbd> on the Mac or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>R</kbd> on Linux, or choose **Resume a conversation…** in the
   command palette.
2. The list shows your conversations, newest first: the prompt that started each one, then the
   agent, its folder and how long ago it was last used. Type in **Filter conversations** to find one
   by its words, folder or id; older ones are found too, and **Show older** loads more.
3. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> (<kbd>Return</kbd> on the Mac) to resume the selected one, or
   <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>Return</kbd> (<kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>Enter</kbd> on Linux) to fork it. A right-click
   on a row offers **Resume** and **Fork this conversation** too.

- **Resume** opens a new tab running the agent in that conversation (`claude --resume <id>`,
  `codex resume <id>`, `grok --resume <id>`) in the conversation's folder: in the
  [space](#spaces) working in that folder when there is one, else in the space you're in, and the
  window shows it. If the folder is gone, it opens in the space's folder, or your home folder when the space has none, and says so.
- A conversation a pane already has is marked **●**, in whichever space, and resuming it goes to
  that pane instead of starting it twice. Right after a resume, until AIDE sees the agent running, the row says
  **Starting…**.
- **Fork** starts a new conversation from that one (`claude --resume <id> --fork-session`,
  `codex fork <id>`), in a new tab, even when the conversation is open.
- Runs nobody typed into, such as `claude -p` and `codex exec`, are hidden; **Show automated runs**
  lists them too. **Rescan** reads the conversations again.
- The list reads Claude Code's, Codex's and Grok's own conversation files and nothing leaves your
  computer. agy's conversations aren't listed.

To fork the agent in a pane you're looking at, right-click its row in the Agents list, or choose
**Fork this conversation** in the command palette: the fork keeps the agent's options, such as
`--dangerously-skip-permissions`, and opens in a new tab beside it.

[Agent cards](#cards-linked-to-panes) follow the conversation:

- Resuming a conversation whose pane had a card links that card to the new pane again, when it is
  the only card last linked to that conversation and it isn't done. Otherwise the list says why it
  wasn't: **Not linked: Fix the login is done**, or **Not linked again: 2 cards were on it**.
- A fork is a new conversation, so it starts with no card: **The fork isn't linked to Fix the login**.

### Links in a pane

<kbd>⌘</kbd>-click on the Mac, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>-click on Linux, opens what's under the pointer:

- A web address (`http://` or `https://`) opens in your browser.
- An AIDE link opens in AIDE: a note (`aide://note/<id>`), the Wiki (`aide://wiki`), or a card
  (`aide://card/<vault>/<card>`), which opens on the Board with its details rather than in its pane.
  A program can print anything, so other AIDE links do nothing from a terminal, and these only show
  a place: they never change anything.
- A file and line, such as `src/main.rs:10:5` or `/Users/you/app/File.swift:120:15`, as compilers and
  test runners print them. A relative path is read from the pane's folder. A note of the notes
  folder you have open opens in the editor at that line. Any other file opens in a new pane to the
  right running your editor at that line: `$VISUAL`, else `$EDITOR`, as your shell's login files
  set them (a variable set only in `.zshrc` or `.bashrc` doesn't count), given `+<line>` and the
  path. With neither set, AIDE says to set one and opens nothing. The terminal server starts the
  editor itself, the path only ever its argument, never typed into a shell. A path that isn't a
  file does nothing, and so does one in a remote session, whose files are the other computer's, or
  with a terminal server older than this AIDE.

Links a program writes with OSC 8 work the same way. On the iPhone, tapping an AIDE link in a
session's pane opens it.

### Keys and pictures

Keys with no text, such as <kbd>Home</kbd>, <kbd>F5</kbd> or the arrows, reach programs the way
xterm sends them. A program that asks for the kitty keyboard protocol gets every key with its
modifiers instead, while **Kitty keyboard protocol** is on.

On Linux, dropping files on a tab types their paths, quoted for the shell. A dropped image from
another app is saved first and its path typed; a dropped link is typed as text.

The terminal sets `TERM=xterm-256color`, `COLORTERM=truecolor` and `TERM_PROGRAM=AIDE`.
The terminal server starts each shell with your login shell's environment, read once when
the server starts, so a shell never carries variables of the session AIDE happened to start in. A
program in the terminal can copy to your clipboard but can't read it.

Each pane's shell also gets `COPAD_SOCKET` and `AIDE_TERMINAL_SOCKET`,
the terminal server's [terminal socket](/docs/developers/local-api/#terminal-socket), and
`COPAD_PANEL_ID` and `AIDE_MUX_PANE`, that pane's id. With them, clicking comux's desktop
notification brings forward the exact tab and pane it came from, and copad's `coctl` keeps working.
[`aidectl terminal`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal) uses the same socket to read, type into,
split and list panes and tabs from a script.

### comux and tmux

comux, tmux and other multiplexers run in a pane like any other program and draw their own screens.
AIDE's own panes take comux's <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>B</kbd> keys through the
[prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key), which is <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>B</kbd> unless you set another.
Pressed twice, the prefix still reaches a comux or tmux running in a pane; once you **Clear** the
prefix, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>B</kbd> reaches it every time.
A comux in a pane can still bring its pane forward when you click one of
its notifications, through the terminal socket above.

### Open remote AIDE…

Open another computer's AIDE terminal sessions in this window, beside your own tabs, over SSH. The
other computer can be a Mac or Linux machine where AIDE is installed and has been opened once.

1. Right-click a tab and choose **Open remote AIDE…**, or run **Open remote AIDE…** from the
   command palette (the action `terminal/open-remote-aide`, which you can give a shortcut in
   [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/guides/shortcuts/)).
2. Enter a host from `~/.ssh/config` or `user@host`, or pick one of your `~/.ssh/config` hosts
   under the field, and choose **Connect**.
3. **Sessions on** the host (**Sessions on box**, say) lists its sessions with their tab count and tab names, and whether one
   is already open in this window or another. Pick one.

The session's tabs open after your own, each with a globe and the host's name before its title.

- What you type, splits, new tabs (**+** or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>T</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>T</kbd>
  on Linux, while one of its tabs is selected), renames and closes happen on that computer, in that
  session. The shells are that computer's, with its environment.
- A session is open in one window at most. Opening it again brings that window and its tabs
  forward.
- **End session…**, beside a session in the list, ends its shells on that computer, after asking.
- **Disconnect from** the host (**Disconnect from box**), in a remote tab's menu, closes its tabs here. Closing the window or
  quitting AIDE does the same. None of these ends anything on the other computer, whatever **Keep
  shells running after AIDE quits** says: its shells end only when you close a remote tab or pane
  with its × or <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> on Linux), or
  end the session.
- Remote tabs aren't part of this computer's layout and don't come back by themselves. The next
  window offers what was open (**demo on box was open here**, with **Reconnect**); AIDE never connects
  by itself, since SSH may need your key unlocked.

If the connection drops, the tabs keep their last screen under **Disconnected from box** (the host's
name), with **Reconnect**. Reconnecting shows the session as it is now, splits, renames and new tabs another
AIDE made meanwhile included, with each pane's shell still running. A session that ended meanwhile
says so (**demo isn't on box any more**), with **Close**.

A pane shown on two computers has one size: the one you typed or clicked in last sizes it. On the
other one the pane keeps that size, cut off or with empty space around it, and says who sizes it in its
corner (**Sized by mac-mini**). Clicking or typing in the pane gives it this window's size again. This
works the same for your own panes when another AIDE opens them.

SSH is your system's `ssh`, with your own keys, agent and `~/.ssh/config`. AIDE never asks for a
password and never relaxes host key checking:

- A host whose key isn't in `known_hosts` is refused: connect to it once with `ssh` yourself to
  check and accept its key.
- A host whose key changed is refused with `ssh`'s own warning.
- A host where AIDE hasn't been set up says **AIDE isn't set up on box: open AIDE there once**.

The other computer treats the connection as remote: nothing running there can bring windows
forward on this computer, and none of your local environment reaches its shells, except what your
SSH login there has (below).

#### Your agent over SSH

A tab or pane you open on the other computer takes the variables in
**Variables from the attaching client** from the SSH login AIDE connected with, as tmux's
`update-environment` does. With `ForwardAgent yes` for that host in your `~/.ssh/config`, its
`SSH_AUTH_SOCK` is your forwarded agent, so `git push` there signs with the keys on this computer.

- It applies to tabs and panes opened afterwards: by you, or by `aidectl` or a layout file on that
  computer, which take what the last AIDE to connect gave. Tabs already open keep what they started
  with; `eval "$(aidectl terminal env)"` in one brings it up to date.
- A variable the connection doesn't have keeps the other computer's own value: on a Mac, its own
  SSH agent.
- A forwarded agent works while that connection is open. After **Disconnect** or a dropped
  connection, the tabs opened through it point at an agent that's gone; **Reconnect**, then open a
  new tab or run `eval "$(aidectl terminal env)"`.
- The same list applies to AIDE on your own computer: a tab you open takes your session's agent and
  display. `PATH`, `HOME`, `SHELL`, `LD_*` and the other variables a shell or the terminal depends on
  are never taken, even when you add them to the list.

### Coding agents

AIDE knows when a coding agent runs in a pane, what it's doing, and when it finishes or needs you. You
see it in the Agents list, on its tab and in the status items, and you get a notification when you
aren't looking at it. You can also say a prompt to it: see [Voice prompts](/docs/guides/voice-prompts/).

AIDE recognises the program in front in a pane by its name: Claude Code (`claude`), Codex (`codex`),
and `agy`, `grok`, `aider`, `cursor`, `gemini`, `opencode`, `droid`, `copilot`, `qwen` and `crush`.
Claude Code's state comes from the session file it keeps; every other agent's is read from what's on
its screen. For Claude Code and Codex, AIDE also reads their own logs for the step they're on (the
command they run, the file they edit) and how full their context is. Nothing is sent anywhere to do
this, and no program is started.

An agent is in one of these states:

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Working** | It is busy with a turn. |
| **Done** | Its turn ended while you weren't looking. It becomes **Waiting** once you look at the pane. |
| **Waiting** | Its turn ended and you've seen it; it waits for your next prompt. |
| **Needs you** | It asks you something: a permission or a question. |
| **Idle** | An agent is in front, and nothing says what it's doing. |

#### The Agents list

The Terminal section has a panel of its own, beside the tabs, which **Toggle Panel** shows and hides
as for your notes. Under **Agents** it lists the agents in this window's tabs, with the number that
need you beside the heading. Each row shows the agent's state and how long it has been in it, the
tab's name, and under it what it asks while it needs you, else the step it's on, else the last line
it printed, with a small gauge of its context when AIDE knows it.

- The agents that need you come first, then the rest in tab order.
- It lists the agents in every [space](#spaces), not only the one on
  screen: once you have more than one space, each has a heading with its name, the space on screen
  first, then the spaces with an agent that needs you. Clicking an agent in another space switches
  the window to that space first.
- Agents working in the same git checkout (the same folder of a
  repository, a linked worktree counting as its own) show the git mark and how many share it, as a
  reminder that they edit the same files.
- An agent whose pane works on an [agent card](/docs/guides/agent-board/)
  has a third line, **on:** with the card's title. Right-click the row and choose **Open card** to
  see the card on **Todos › Board**. A tab's tooltip names its pane's card the same way.
  The line also shows how many of the card's steps are done, such as
  **2/4**, and clicking it opens the card (the rest of the row still opens the pane).
- A remote session's agents (see [Open remote AIDE…](#open-remote-aide)) are listed under the
  host's name.
- A row that says **Not updating** is one AIDE couldn't read just now; it shows the last state it
  knows.
- Right-click a Claude Code agent's row and choose **Compact at next
  step** to have it compact before its next model request (see
  [below](#hold-claude-codes-auto-compaction-until-you-commit)). The row shows **⇣** before its
  context gauge until it does; **Cancel compact** takes the request back. **Compact at next step** in
  the command palette does the same for the agent in the focused pane.
- Click a row to go to that agent: its tab is selected, its pane gets the keyboard and the window
  comes forward. Looking at it this way turns **Done** into **Waiting**.
- With none, the list says **No agents running**.
- Click a space's or host's heading to fold it; a folded heading still
  shows how many of its agents need you (▲), are done (✓) and are working (●). Going to a space opens
  it until you leave it again; a heading you fold or open yourself stays that way.
- The context gauge turns to the warning colour once the context is 80%
  full or more.
- The row of an agent that needs you has an **Open inline** button: the
  right panel shows **Agents** › **Focused** on that agent with its pane's last lines, read again every
  two seconds, and **Open pane** to go there (**Can't read this pane** when they don't come). Where the
  pane's terminal server can't send its lines, or the panel has no Agents view, the button is **Open**
  and goes to the pane as a click on the row does. Neither shows while the row says **Not updating**.

The panel shows a **Tabs** list by default, instead of the Agents list
above. To get the Agents list back, set **Settings › Terminal › Layout › Left list** to **Agents** (on
Linux also `left = ["agents"]` under `[terminal.layout]` in `config.toml`). The Agents list was the
default before, so choosing it then saved nothing: choose it again after the update. The Tabs list shows every tab of every space under its space's heading, with
its agent's state, and a split tab's agents under it. A tab with no agent shows the folder its shell
is in under its name, when AIDE knows it and the row has two lines (`~/dev/aide`; a remote host's and
the iPhone's as the whole path). On the Mac that's the tabs of the space the window shows. Spaces with no agent, no unread notice and no
bell fold into one **Quiet · N spaces** row, which you click to list them; the space on screen never
folds, and a space leaves the fold as soon as one of those arrives.

While an agent needs you, both lists start with a **Needs you** group: the agents waiting for your
answer, then those that finished while you weren't looking. One finished agent is listed and the
others are summed up as **N more finished**, with their spaces; while you filter, every match is
listed. Each row names its space and tab, and
does what that agent's own row further down does, and is tinted orange. A finished one leaves the
group once you look at it. The group's heading always shows how many need you, and it folds like a
space.

#### Answer a prompt without going to the pane

When Claude Code, started in a terminal pane, asks before it runs a
command, fetches a page or searches the web, or asks you a question with one answer, its row shows
what it asks: the command or address in a monospaced line, then **Allow once** and **Deny**, or one
button per answer for a question (hold the pointer over one for its description). The buttons show
once, in **Needs you** while that group is open, and **Open** stays beside them.

- **Allow once** allows that one call. Claude asks again the next time; nothing is added to its
  permission rules. **Deny** tells Claude "Denied in AIDE".
- **Agents** › **Focused** in the right panel shows the prompt whole with its buttons, also when it's
  too long for a row.
- Edits, new files, other tools, a command with its own time limit, a question that takes several
  answers or comes with other questions, and anything too long to show whole get **Open** only:
  answer those in the pane.
- The pane's own dialog still works. Whichever answer reaches Claude first counts. When your answer
  goes through you see **Answer sent**. When it can't (the prompt changed, or the connection
  dropped), AIDE says why, for example **The prompt changed. Answer it in the pane.**, and on the
  Mac and Linux the pane opens.
- It works for sessions AIDE starts; AIDE adds its hook for those sessions alone and never changes
  Claude Code's own settings. `claude -p` runs without it, as no one is at a dialog.
- With a terminal server from before this release, prompts show **Open** only.
- On the iPhone, the **Needs you** rows of a host's AIDE sessions show the same buttons. There, a
  press that sends nothing shows why on the list and leaves the row's **Open** to reach the pane.

Above the rows, **Filter spaces, tabs, agents** narrows both lists as
you type. It matches space and tab names, what an agent is doing and its card's title, and opens the
spaces that match; in the Tabs list, quiet ones too. The chips under it, **All**, **Needs you**, **Agents** and
**Working**, each with its count, narrow it further. **Esc** in the field clears it, and ⌘F (Ctrl+F on
Linux) goes to the field while the keyboard is in the list; in a pane it still finds in the pane. The
filter is per window and isn't kept when you close it. With nothing left, the list says **No
matches**.

The other settings there (on Linux, the keys under `[terminal.layout]`):

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Fold quiet spaces** (`fold_quiet`) | On | Off lists the quiet spaces like the others. |
| **Rows** (`row_lines`, `auto_lines`) | Auto | **Auto** draws a row on up to three lines (its title, what it's doing, its card), and on one line while the list is longer than the panel; the selected tab's row and an agent waiting for your answer keep theirs. **One line**, **Two lines** or **Three lines** draw a row on at most that many lines, however long the list. A one-line row shows the context gauge whenever its context is known. A `row_lines` set in `config.toml` from before stays a fixed count. |
| **Row format** (`row_format`) | the title | A template for a row's title over `glyph`, `title`, `action`, `card`, `context` and `space`, written as the status bar's **Tab format** is. One that doesn't read shows its line and column under the field and is kept as you wrote it, while the list uses the title. |

On Linux, a setting you put back to its default in Settings is taken out of `config.toml`, so a later
version's default reaches it; a value you write into the file yourself stays until you change it.
[`aidectl terminal-layout`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#terminal-layout) reads these settings as AIDE does.

#### Jump to an agent

Two commands take you to an agent wherever it is, switching the window to its space when needed:

| Command | Mac | Linux | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jump to the agent that needs you** | <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>B</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>B</kbd> | The agent that has needed you longest. Press it again from there for the next one. |
| **Jump to the newest finished agent** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>U</kbd> | none (in the command palette) | The agent that most recently finished or started needing you, and that you haven't looked at. Press it again for the one before. |

With nothing to go to, the Terminal section says **No agent needs you** or **Nothing unread**. The
counts on the [status bar](#the-status-bar) do the same when you click them.

#### Agent overview

The agent overview lists every agent at once, so you can see which one
needs you and why without going through the tabs. On the Mac and Linux, click the working count on
the [status bar](#the-status-bar) or choose **Agent overview** (<kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> on
the Mac, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>A</kbd> on Linux). The same list is on
**Todos › Board** under **Agents**, on the iPhone too (see [The Board](/docs/guides/todos/#the-board)).

It covers the agents in every space and on every computer the window is connected to, grouped as
**Needs you**, **Done**, **Stalled**, **Working**, **Waiting**, **Idle** and **Agent left**. Each row
shows:

- the agent, and the [card](/docs/guides/agent-board/) its pane works on with its steps, such as
  **Fix login · 2/5**;
- what it asks while it needs you, such as **Approve: Bash: make test**, else what it's doing. A card
  an agent moved to **Waiting** with a question shows that question, even after the agent has quit;
- **No progress for 12m** when an agent has been working for 10 minutes without writing anything to
  its conversation (or, for an agent AIDE reads only from its screen, printing anything): it may be
  stuck, or running something long;
- its space and checkout, how full its context is, what it has cost, and what its checkout changed
  against the last commit, such as **+120 −14 · 6 files** (new files count too; **No changes** for
  none);
- **Same files as aide-wt: src/a.rs, src/b.rs** when another agent's worktree of the same repository
  changed some of the same files, a sign that their work may conflict.

Each row has **Open pane**, **Open card** while it works on a card, and **Peek**, which shows the
pane's last lines and keeps them current, so you can check on an agent without leaving where you
are. In the terminal's overview, <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> pick a row, <kbd>Return</kbd> opens its
pane, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes the overview. With no agents it says **No agents are running.**

A tab with an agent that is working, done or needs you shows a small mark next to its name: a dot
while it works, a check when it's done, and a warning sign while it needs you. Point at the mark to
read the agent and its state.

The [status items](/docs/guides/appearance/#status-items-mac-and-linux) at the bottom of the sidebar
also count the agents that need you or are done.

#### Notifications

When an agent finishes or needs you, AIDE posts a notification, **Claude finished · build** or
**Claude needs you · build** (the agent, then its tab), with what it asks when it's waiting on you, Codex's last message when it
finishes, or else just **Turn finished** or **Waiting for you**. There is
one notification for each time, however many AIDE windows show the pane, and none while you're
looking at the pane: AIDE in front with that pane on screen. A notification for the same pane
replaces the one before.

Click it to go to the pane: AIDE selects its tab, gives the pane the keyboard, brings the window
forward and marks the agent as seen. A pane closed since says **That terminal has closed**.
An agent in a space no window shows gets a notification too, and its
click switches the window in front to that space.

Desktop notifications, for agents and for the ones you post with [`aidectl notify`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#notify), are posted only while **Settings › Terminal › Agents › Desktop notifications** is on (it's on unless you turn it off). That goes for this computer's and for those of computers you opened with **Open remote AIDE…**, whose agents show in the Agents list either way. The switch changes only what reaches the desktop: each computer's terminal server keeps its own notifications whatever it says, and this computer's are in the [notification center](#notification-center).

The first notification asks for permission on the Mac. On Linux the desktop may keep the window from
coming forward; the pane is selected all the same.

#### Notification center

The notification center keeps the notifications your agents raised on
this computer, the last 200, so you can catch up on what you missed. Right-click an agent count on
the [status bar](#the-status-bar) and choose **Notifications**, or choose **Notifications** in the
command palette. While a terminal has the keyboard, <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> on the Mac,
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> on Linux, or the [prefix key](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#the-prefix-key) then
<kbd>a</kbd> opens it too.

- Each row shows the agent's mark, the notification's title, and what it said with how long ago,
  newest first; the ones you haven't read are in bold.
- <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> pick a row, <kbd>Return</kbd> (or a click) goes to its pane, switching
  the window to its space when needed, and marks it read. <kbd>Delete</kbd> (or **Dismiss** in the
  row's menu) takes one out, **Clear all** takes out every one, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes it.
- Going to a pane any other way reads its notifications too. Reading the notification of an agent's
  latest turn counts as looking at the agent, so it stops showing as **Done**; an older one doesn't.
- A pane closed since says **That pane is gone**. With none it says **No notifications**.
- It keeps every agent's finished and needs-you notification, also
  for a kind whose **Notify when …** switch is off, and the ones you post yourself with
  [`aidectl notify`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#notify), under the title you gave them.
- It shows this computer's notifications only. On the iPhone, the system's notification list and the
  **Agents** sheet do this job.
- `aidectl terminal notices` lists the same notifications.

#### Set up agent hooks…

Without hooks, AIDE notices an agent finishing or needing you within about a second, and waits a few
seconds after a turn ends to be sure. With them, Claude Code and Codex tell AIDE the moment it
happens, and Claude Code also reports how full its context is.

**Set up agent hooks…** shows what it would change first, under **Set up agent hooks?**: each file,
where it links to when it's a symlink, and the lines that change. Nothing is written until you choose
**Apply**.

- Claude Code's `~/.claude/settings.json` gets a `Stop` and a `Notification` hook, and its
  `statusLine` runs through AIDE first. A status line you already have is kept and still runs, with
  the same output.
- Codex's `~/.codex/config.toml` (or `$CODEX_HOME/config.toml`) gets AIDE in its `notify` program,
  which then runs the one you had.
- A tool that isn't set up on this computer is skipped. Running it again says **Nothing to change.**
- Each file is backed up beside itself first (`settings.json.aide-backup-<time>`), and a file that
  changed since you saw the diff isn't written.

Claude Code started in an AIDE pane already gets the `Stop` and `Notification` hooks for that session, with nothing written to your files, while **Give agents AIDE's context** is on. Setting up hooks still adds Codex's `notify` program, which those sessions don't get, and Claude Code's `statusLine`, which reports how full its context is.

The hooks only do anything in AIDE's own panes. Elsewhere, or inside comux, they run what you had
and nothing else. [`aidectl agent install-hooks`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent) does the same from a
terminal, and takes them out again with `--remove`.

#### Check setup…

**Check setup…** looks over what the terminal on this computer depends on
and shows what it finds under **Setup check**, as `aidectl terminal doctor` and `aide-mux doctor` do
in a shell (the way `comux doctor` did). It changes nothing.

- **Terminal server**: whether it runs, and whether it's from this AIDE. One from another version, or
  one too old to answer, has **Restart it…**.
- **Sockets**: the terminal server's folder and sockets are yours and closed to other users.
- **Terminal settings**: the terminal server's `config.json` reads, and any key in it that AIDE
  doesn't know.
- **Sessions**, **Agents**, **Usage** and **Phone push**: how big the saved sessions are, how many
  panes and agents there are, each usage reading's last problem, and whether notifications reach a
  phone. Agent detection that stopped has **Restart it…**.
- **Agent hooks**: whether Claude Code's and Codex's hooks are in place. **Claude Code hooks: not
  installed** has **Install hooks…**, which opens [Set up agent hooks…](#set-up-agent-hooks) with its
  diff first.
- **Agent skills**: whether the [plugin skills](#plugin-skills) are up to date.
- **Last restore**: each program the terminal server started again after it restarted, and whether
  it came back (by its name only), with what a restore never keeps.
- **State folder**, **Local API** and **This computer**: how much the terminal server keeps, whether
  an AIDE app answers [aidectl](/docs/developers/aidectl/), and whether `git` is installed.

Each finding is marked as fine, a note, a warning or an error, with a line on what it means.

#### Hold Claude Code's auto-compaction until you commit

AIDE can hold Claude Code's automatic compaction while the work tree has uncommitted changes to
tracked files, so a compacted conversation doesn't lose track of work that isn't committed yet.
Claude carries on uncompacted and asks again before its next request. It's added only with
[`aidectl agent install-hooks --pre-compact`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent), as a `PreCompact`
hook.

- Claude Code compacts close to the end of its context by default, too late for this. Set
  `CLAUDE_AUTOCOMPACT_PCT_OVERRIDE` (the percentage of the context at which it compacts, such as
  `70`) in the environment Claude Code runs in. AIDE doesn't set it.
- It holds only an automatic compaction, never `/compact`; only while there's room left (60,000
  tokens beyond a tenth of the context); and at most 40 times in a row. Untracked files don't count.
- Whenever it isn't sure (no AIDE pane, an old reading, `git` slow or failing, not a repository), it
  lets Claude compact. It answers within a second and a half.

In request mode the gate holds every automatic compaction until you ask
for one, so a long task or a loop isn't compacted in the middle of a step. Ask with **Compact at next
step** on the agent's row (in the terminal's **Agents** list, or the iPhone's **Agents** sheet), or
with `aidectl terminal compact`. Claude compacts at its next model request, even in the middle of a
turn; nothing is typed into the pane.

- Install it with `aidectl agent install-hooks --pre-compact=request`. It also sets
  `CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_COMPACT_WINDOW` (your model's context window, which it asks for, or `--window`)
  and `CLAUDE_AUTOCOMPACT_PCT_OVERRIDE` to `5` in `settings.json`'s `env`, so Claude asks the gate
  before almost every request. A value you set there already is left alone. Removing the hooks takes
  the `5` out again and leaves the window. Installing one mode over the other replaces AIDE's gate
  (going back to `--pre-compact` takes the `5` out too); a gate you edited is left alone, with a note.
- A request is used once, by the next automatic compaction (or a `/compact`). It goes when the
  conversation changes (`/clear`, a new agent in the pane).
- It still lets Claude compact when fewer than 60,000 tokens are left, measured from the
  conversation's own file. When it can't tell how full the context is, it allows one compaction
  every 10 minutes and holds the rest.

#### Cards linked to panes

When a coding agent in one of AIDE's panes adds an [agent card](/docs/guides/agent-board/) with `aidectl board add`, or picks one up with `aidectl board link`, the card is linked to that pane. A pane has one card at a time, and on one computer a card is linked to one pane: linking it from another pane moves it there.

- The link lasts as long as the agent and its conversation. A new conversation (`/clear`), another agent in the pane or the agent quitting finishes it. A finished link still names the pane, so you can open it while the pane is there, but it no longer shows that agent's state.
- It survives the terminal server restarting, and comes back finished: Claude Code resumed in the same conversation (`claude --resume`) takes it up again. An in-place update of the terminal server keeps it as it is.
- The terminal socket's `agent.list` names each pane's card beside its agent (see the [terminal socket](/docs/developers/local-api/#terminal-socket)).
- **Start in a worktree…** on a card (its menu on the Board, or its
  details) makes a branch for the card, `<project>/<card>-<title>`, in a worktree beside the
  repository, and a space there (see [Worktrees](#worktrees)). Every shell in that space has
  `AIDE_BOARD_CARD`, so an agent you start there that runs `aidectl board add` or `aidectl board link`
  works on that card; AIDE never links it for the agent. The space pill's tooltip and its row in
  **Spaces** name the card, and ending the space with its worktree asks whether to move the card to
  **Waiting**, where you review it.
- A card link, `aide://card/<vault>/<card>`, opens the card's pane: on the Mac and Linux when one of the window's terminals shows it, on the iPhone when one of the [hosts' AIDE sessions](#aide-sessions-on-a-host) you have open does. Elsewhere the card's note opens at the card, saying **Not open on this device**. With the [agent board](/docs/guides/agent-board/) on, the Board opens with the card selected instead, and the note opens only when the card isn't on the board. A link to a card of another vault says **This card is in a vault that isn't open**.
- A card link you click in a pane opens the card on the Board, never a pane (see [Links in a pane](#links-in-a-pane)).
- `<vault>` is the vault's id: a notes folder's own id, or `server:` followed by the server's and your user's ids for a server's notes.

#### AIDE's context for agents

When you start Claude Code or Codex in a pane, it already knows it's in AIDE: where your notes are,
how to keep its plan on the [agent board](/docs/guides/agent-board/), and which card the pane works
on. Nothing is written to the agents' own settings: the context is for that session only, and an
agent you start anywhere else runs as it always does.

- **Claude Code** starts with AIDE's plugin `aide` for the session: the `aide:aide-board` skill, and
  a short note at the start of each session naming the pane, the pane's own `aidectl` and its card.
  Your own plugins, MCP servers and hooks stay as they are.
- **Codex** gets a few lines of instructions after any of your own `developer_instructions` (from
  the command line, the profile you chose or `config.toml`), pointing it at `aidectl guide board`
  for the details. Your `AGENTS.md` files are read as usual. A project whose own
  `.codex/config.toml` sets `developer_instructions` is left as it is.
- Only the commands that start a session get it: `claude` and `codex`, `codex exec`, `codex resume`
  and `codex fork`. `claude mcp`, `codex login` and the like run untouched.
- It reaches agents you resume or fork, and the ones that come back after a restart.
- It's given only when AIDE's terminal server confirms the agent was started inside that pane, and
  only while **Settings › Terminal › Give agents AIDE's context** is on. Turn it off there: the next
  agent you start, in any pane, runs without it.
- An agent you start over SSH outside an AIDE pane gets nothing (a [remote pane](#open-remote-aide) is an
  AIDE pane and does get it). On such a computer, use
  [`aidectl agent skills sync`](/docs/developers/aidectl/#agent-skills-on-a-computer-without-the-app) for the skills and `aidectl agent
  install-hooks` for the agent hooks on that computer instead.

It works because the pane puts AIDE's own `claude` and `codex` first on `PATH`, ahead of even
`aidectl`; they add the context and start the real program. So an alias or a shell function named
`claude` or `codex`, or the program's full path, skips AIDE. `command claude` goes through AIDE
again.

Run `aidectl where` in a pane to see whether the pane can give agents the context, and why not. It
also names an alias or a shell function that skips AIDE. An `on` there isn't a promise: a version of
Claude Code or Codex that doesn't have the option AIDE uses still starts without it, and so does
Codex in a project whose `.codex/config.toml` sets `developer_instructions`. Run `aidectl where` inside the agent to see
what AIDE decided when that session started.

#### Plugin skills

Claude Code and Codex started in a pane learn the board for that session
without this, while **Give agents AIDE's context** is on: Claude Code as the skill, Codex as a pointer
to `aidectl guide board` (see [AIDE's context for agents](#aides-context-for-agents)). These skills
are for the other plugins, for Grok, and for agents you start outside AIDE. On the Mac and on Linux, AIDE can give Claude Code, Codex and Grok the skills of the plugins you turn
on: short instructions that teach an agent how to use a plugin with `aidectl`. It's off until you
turn on **Give plugins' skills to your coding agents** under **Settings › Terminal › Plugin skills**.

| Plugin | Skill | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Agent board** | `aide-board` | Keeping its plan on the [agent board](/docs/guides/agent-board/) with `aidectl board`. Given while the board is on. |
| **Browser** (core plugin) | `aide-browser` | Driving AIDE's browser with `aidectl browser` and `aidectl secret`. |
| **Terminal** | `aide-terminal` | Watching other panes: reading them, waiting for a line or for another agent to finish or ask something (`aidectl terminal wait-output`, `wait-agent`), and following events; and reporting to the agent that started it, or reading its own workers' reports, through the panes' inboxes (`aidectl terminal message --to owner`, `inbox`). It only reads, waits and leaves messages; it treats what it reads as data. Given in every workspace. |
| **GitHub**, **Jira**, **Linear** | `aide-github`, `aide-jira`, `aide-linear` | Reading your open work from the plugin's section of `work/now`. They only read; they can't change anything in the tool. |

- Turning it on shows what would change first, under **Give plugins' skills to your coding agents?**:
  each skill and the folder it goes to, with its text. Nothing is written until you choose
  **Apply**; **Cancel** turns it off again.
- After that, a skill comes and goes with its plugin. Turn the Browser on and `aide-browser` is
  added; turn it off and it's taken out. The plugin's own page says so: **Adds the skill aide-browser
  for Claude Code and Codex.** On Linux that line is under the Browser on the **Core plugins** page,
  and in a server plugin's row.
- Each skills folder that's on this computer is listed, and you can leave one out: **Claude Code's
  skills folder** (`~/.claude/skills`, or `$CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR/skills`), **Codex's skills folder**
  (`~/.codex/skills`, or `$CODEX_HOME/skills`) and **Grok's skills folder** (`~/.grok/skills`). Grok
  also reads Claude Code's folder unless you turned that off in Grok, so that row says **(also read
  by Grok)**.
- Each skill ends with a section **Where this works**: the vaults and servers where its plugin is on.
  The agent passes `--vault` or `--server` on every `aidectl` command, and asks you which one when
  there's more than one.
- A skill you edited is left as you made it, and the list says **Edited by you in Claude Code: left
  alone**. A skill of your own with the same name is never touched. Turning the switch off takes out
  what AIDE added, except those.
- A session that's already running may still remember a skill that was taken out. The plugin's own
  commands refuse to act where the plugin is off, whatever the agent remembers.

##### Skills in a community plugin

A [community plugin](/docs/plugins/device-plugins/) can bring up to three skills of its own. They're
off until you agree to them on the plugin's page, also when it comes from the index.

- Under **Skills for your coding agents**, turn on **Give its skills to your coding agents**. The line
  under it names the skills and the agents that will read them. You can turn it on while installing,
  too.
- **Read them** shows each skill as your agents will see it, with "Your agents will read these
  instructions and may follow them with your permissions." AIDE adds the **Where this works**
  section at the end.
- The page also flags what's worth a second look, without blocking it: a site the plugin doesn't ask
  to connect to, `curl`, `wget`, `ssh` or `rm`, mentions of `~/.ssh`, `.env`, tokens, keys,
  passwords or secrets, and `aidectl` commands beyond the access the plugin asks for.
- You agree to the text. An update with the same text keeps your answer, and so does going back to
  a version you agreed to. When an update changes the text, AIDE takes the skill out and the page
  says **Its skills changed since you agreed: read them again.**
- A skill is given only while its plugin runs: turning the plugin off, removing it or
  **Restricted mode** takes it out. When two workspaces have the same skill with different text
  (different versions of one plugin), neither is given, and the Plugin skills list says which.
- AIDE gives your agents at most 16 skills.
- The iPhone has no local agents: it installs such a plugin and leaves its skills out.

The Terminal is part of the Mac app on the release page. A build for the Mac App Store, where the
sandbox doesn't allow starting a shell, would leave it out.

## SSH on the iPhone

<Aside type="caution">
The iPhone app is in a TestFlight beta and isn't public yet.
</Aside>

SSH is a built-in feature you turn on in **Settings › Core plugins › SSH** ("Saved hosts,
keys and passwords, terminals and AIDE sessions on your servers."). It adds an **SSH** section: open sessions on top,
then your saved hosts by group, with a search field.

On an iPad at full width, **SSH** is on the ribbon, where the Mac has the
terminal.

### Add a host

Choose **+** (**Add host**) and fill in:

- **Label**: what the list shows, such as "My server".
- **Host** (`example.com` or `192.168.0.2`), **Port** (22 by default) and **User**.
- **Group**, to sort hosts in the list (optional).
- **Sign in with**: **A password, asked each time**, or a key or saved password from your keychain.
  **Manage keychain…** opens the keychain.
- **Startup command**, such as `tmux attach`: offered in the session's menu as **Run startup
  command**, never run on its own.

Tap a host to connect. The session has its own terminal with the keyboard's accessory row, and a
menu with **Run startup command**, **Disconnect** and **Reconnect**. Sessions stay open while you
move around the app, and switching between notes on the device and a server doesn't close them;
turning SSH off does.

### AIDE sessions on a host

If AIDE is installed on a host (a Mac or Linux machine where you've opened AIDE once), its row in the
list has an **AIDE** button beside it. It opens that computer's AIDE terminal sessions, the
same ones its own Terminal shows, over the same sign-in, known hosts and keychain as a shell.
Touching and holding the row offers **Open AIDE** too.

1. Tap **AIDE**. **AIDE on** the host (**AIDE on box**, say) lists its sessions, each with its name
   and tab count.
2. Pick a session. Its tabs run across the top, with **+** for **New tab**, and the tab you pick
   shows one pane at a time.
3. Type. What you type, paste and tap goes to that pane's shell on the other computer.

The screens follow the Mac's and Linux's terminal:

- **Sessions** says how many spaces and tabs the host has, and **Search spaces, tabs, agents**
  narrows it as you type. First, under **Needs you**, the agents waiting for your answer with
  **Open**, then the first of those that finished while you weren't looking, with
  **N more finished** to list the rest; then the host's own session (its
  vault's, else its first) with its tabs, each with what its agent is doing and, from 80%, how full
  its context is; then **Other spaces**, one row each, or, while you search, with their matching
  tabs under them.
- In a session a short bar under the terminal takes the place of the tabs across the top: each
  tab's number and its agent's mark, with a thin line as long as the share of the context used
  (green, orange from 80%), **+N** for the tabs that don't fit, a bell with how many agents and
  notifications want you, and the clock. Swipe the bar sideways for the next or previous tab. The title names the
  space, the tab and the card the tab works on.
- Swipe the bar up, or tap the bell, for a sheet with **Agents** (how many are working, need you, are unseen
  and idle; those that need you; this tab's agent with its context and card; then every agent, with
  the actions below), **Notifications** (the host's notification center, from a host on this
  version; tap one to open its pane, or **That terminal has closed** when its pane is
  gone) and **Today** (today's plan).
- The switcher button (⇧⌘O with a keyboard) opens [the switcher](#switch-go-back-mark) as on the Mac:
  **Spaces**, **Agents** and **Tabs** with their counts, the agents in groups.

- A tab split into panes shows its focused pane; **Panes** in the bar lists the others to switch to.
  The iPhone shows splits other computers make, but doesn't split itself.
- The **Session** menu has **New tab**, **Rename tab…**, **Close pane** and **Reconnect**. **Close
  pane** asks first, and names the program still running there.
- The list includes the host's [spaces](#spaces), each with its git
  branch. **+** makes one: type the folder's whole path on the host (the folders its shells are in
  are offered), and its name, which is filled in from the folder. <Badge text="Next release" /> The
  offered folders narrow as you type, the way the Mac's **New space…** finds them, with the host's
  spaces' folders among them; tap one to fill in the folder. Swipe a row for **Rename**; a
  space also has **End**, which asks first. A vault's own session can't be ended from the iPhone.
- Leaving the screen or the app only lets go of the session: its shells keep running on the other
  computer. Coming back shows each pane as it is now.
- A pane has one size. Opening it on the iPhone doesn't change it: if a computer sized it last,
  the iPhone shows it at that size, cut off or with space around it, with **Sized by** that
  computer (**Sized by mac-mini**) and **Fit to this phone**. Typing, pasting, tapping into the
  pane or **Fit to this phone** gives it the iPhone's size until that computer takes it back.
- Programs that use the mouse get your taps as clicks and a two-finger drag as the scroll wheel.
- Pictures that programs draw in the terminal (kitty images) aren't shown on the iPhone.
- The terminal draws in JetBrains Mono Nerd Font, which the app
  includes, so prompt icons and powerline arrows show as they do on the Mac.
- When a program copies (OSC 52, as tmux, Neovim and many CLIs do),
  the text goes to the iPhone's clipboard, as it does in a plain SSH shell. Programs can't read the
  clipboard.
- A bell in the pane on screen gives a light tap. A tab whose pane
  rang while you were elsewhere shows **!** until you open it, and so does its session in the list.
- Touch and hold a session in the list, or an agent in **Agents**, to
  see the last lines of its pane before you open it.
- A paste larger than the pane can take now is refused whole: **That paste is too large to send**.
  When a program stops reading what you type, the pane says **Not responding** until it reads again.
- A tab you open from the iPhone takes the host's SSH login variables
  (its `SSH_CONNECTION`, say), never the iPhone's own; see
  [Your agent over SSH](#your-agent-over-ssh). The iPhone doesn't forward an SSH agent, so on a Mac
  host the tab keeps that Mac's own agent.

**Resume a conversation** over the sessions lists the host's past
conversations, as [the Mac and Linux do](#reopen-a-past-conversation). Tap one to resume it in a new tab
on the host; touch and hold it to **Fork this conversation**.

Coding agents in the host's panes show here too, as the host's AIDE reports them:

- A tab's mark shows its most urgent agent: working, done or needs you. **Panes** names each pane's
  agent and what it's doing.
- A session's row adds what needs you, such as "1 needs you, 2 done".
- **Agents** (beside the list, and in the **Session** menu) lists the host's agents across its
  sessions, those that need you first, each with its tab, what it's waiting for or doing, and how
  full its context is. Tapping one opens its pane. One whose pane
  works on an agent card says **on:** with the card's title; touch and hold it for **Open card**.
  The line shows the card's steps done (**2/4**), and tapping it
  opens the card.
  Touch and hold a Claude Code agent for **Compact at next step** (or
  **Cancel compact**), when the host's AIDE has it; the row shows **⇣** until it compacts.
- An agent counts as seen once its pane is on the iPhone's screen while you're in the app: a
  finished turn stops showing as done. Being in a list, or a turn that finishes while AIDE is in
  the background, doesn't count. Looking doesn't take the pane's size either.

While it connects, the screen says **Connecting to box…**. If the connection drops, it says
**Disconnected from box**, with **Reconnect**. A connection that goes
quiet without closing (moving from Wi-Fi to cellular, say) is noticed within 30 seconds, without
waiting for you to type, and AIDE connects again by itself once. A host where AIDE hasn't been set up says **AIDE isn't
set up on box: open AIDE there once**, and one whose AIDE is older than the app says **AIDE on box is
older than this app: update it there**.

In the host's editor, the **AIDE** section has **AIDE command**: what runs on the host for its
sessions. Left empty, AIDE runs the AIDE installed there. The section also has **Agent
notifications** (below).

### Agent notifications

**Agent notifications**, in the host editor's **AIDE** section, is on by default. The next time you
open that host's AIDE sessions, AIDE asks for permission to notify you and gives the host's AIDE a
key made for this iPhone. It then sends its agents' notifications through a push relay to Apple's
push service, sealed with that key: only this iPhone can read the title and text. The relay and
Apple carry only a placeholder. If the host's AIDE turns notifications down, or is too old for
them, the section says so, such as **Update AIDE on this host to get notifications.**

Tapping a notification opens the SSH section on that host's AIDE sessions, connects, and shows the
agent's session at its tab and pane. If that pane has closed since, the list says **That terminal
has closed**. A notification still sent by a comux an earlier version registered opens the SSH
host list instead.

While you're looking at a pane of the host's AIDE sessions in the app, a notification for that pane
shows no banner; another pane's still does. If its key is gone, the notification reads "Agent
notification" and "Open AIDE to see it." If SSH is turned off, tapping it tells you to turn SSH on
first.

When Apple gives this iPhone a new push address, the next connection to each host registers the
new one and then removes the old one.

Turning the switch off, deleting the host, or changing its address or user deletes its key at once.
The host's AIDE is told to forget this iPhone the next time you connect to it. Until then, a
notification it still sends arrives as "Agent notification", and tapping it does nothing.

### Keys and passwords

**Keychain** (in the section's menu) holds what you sign in with. Choose **Add**, then:

- **Password…**: a password saved under a name.
- **New ED25519 key…**: AIDE makes a key; its private half never leaves the iPhone's keychain. Open
  the key to **Copy public key** or **Share public key**, and add that line to
  `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` on the server.
- **Import a key…**: an ED25519 private key in OpenSSH format (`BEGIN OPENSSH PRIVATE KEY`),
  unencrypted or with a passphrase.
- **New Secure Enclave key…**: where the iPhone or iPad has a Secure
  Enclave, it makes a P-256 key there. Its private half can't be read out, not even by AIDE, and it
  works only on that device. Copy or share its public key the same way.

Keys and passwords are kept in the iPhone's keychain, never in the host list.

### Known hosts

The first time you connect to a host, AIDE shows its key type and fingerprint. Check it with the
server's owner (`ssh-keygen -lf` on the server's host key) before **Trust and connect**. If a
host's key later changes, AIDE stops and shows both: someone may be intercepting the connection,
so choose **Replace the key and connect** only if you know the server was set up again.

**Known hosts** (in the section's menu) lists every key you've trusted; **Forget** removes one.
