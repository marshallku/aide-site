On the Mac and Linux, AIDE has a terminal of its own, so you can keep a shell next to your notes. On
the iPhone, which has no local shell, the SSH section opens terminals to your servers instead.

## Terminal on the Mac and Linux

The **Terminal** section in the sidebar is a row of tabs over a shell, filling the main area.

- **+** or **New tab** opens a shell in a new tab (<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> on the Mac,
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> on Linux), in the folder of the tab you're in, or your
  home folder. It starts as a login shell, so your profile runs.
- Each tab is titled by what the shell sets, else the program's name. **Rename…** in a tab's menu
  gives it your own name; **Close** or its × ends the shell.
- With no tab open, the section says **No shells open**.
- The shells keep running while you're elsewhere in AIDE. Coming back finds them as you left them.

### Split a tab into panes <Badge text="Next release" />

A tab can hold more than one shell side by side. Each part is a pane, with its own shell, and you
split and move between them from the keyboard or the **Go** menu:

| Action | Mac | Linux |
|---|---|---|
| **Split Right** | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> |
| **Split Down** | <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>D</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>E</kbd> |
| **Next Pane** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> |
| **Previous Pane** | <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>Left</kbd> |
| Close the focused pane | <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> |

- The keys work only while a terminal pane has the keyboard. **Go** › **Split Right**,
  **Split Down**, **Next Pane** and **Previous Pane** (on the Mac) are greyed out otherwise.
- A split halves the focused pane. The new pane opens a shell in the focused pane's folder.
- Click a pane to focus it. While a tab has more than one pane, the focused one has an outline in
  the accent colour.
- Drag the line between two panes to resize them.
- Each pane keeps at least 20 columns and 5 rows. A split that would leave less is refused with a
  beep.
- The tab's title, its folder (where a new tab starts) and the find bar follow the focused pane.
- <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Shift</kbd><kbd>W</kbd> on Linux) closes the focused
  pane and ends its shell; on the tab's last pane it closes the tab. The tab's × and **Close** still
  close the whole tab, with every pane in it.

### Beside your notes

The right panel (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>\\</kbd> on
Linux) has a **Terminal** view: the same tabs and the same shells, next to whatever the main area
shows, such as a note. While the main area is the Terminal section, the shells are there and the
panel says so.

For the tab, pane, find and text-size keys, see [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/guides/shortcuts/#terminal).

### Settings › Terminal

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Show Terminal in the sidebar** | On | Turns the Terminal section (and the panel view) on or off. |
| **Shell** | Your login shell | The program a new tab starts. |
| **Close a tab when its shell exits** | On | Off keeps an ended tab open so you can read what it printed. |
| **Theme** | **System (light and dark)** | System uses Catppuccin Latte in light mode and Catppuccin Mocha in dark. Others: Catppuccin Frappé, Catppuccin Macchiato, Dracula, Nord, Tokyo Night, Gruvbox Dark, One Dark, Solarized Dark. |
| **Font** | A Nerd Font if one is installed, else the system's monospace | Any installed font by name. |
| **Size** | 13 | 8 to 32. |
| **Cursor** | **Block** | **Block**, **Bar** or **Underline**. |
| **Blinking cursor** | On | |
| **Use Option as Meta** (Mac) | On | <kbd>⌥</kbd> works as Meta/Alt in the shell instead of typing special characters. |
| **Scrollback** | 10,000 lines | 1,000 to 100,000. |
| **Draw with the GPU** (Mac) <Badge text="Next release" /> | On | Draws the terminal with Metal. Off draws it with Core Text. Applies to new tabs. |
| **Font thickening** (Mac) <Badge text="Next release" /> | **Off** | **On**, **Off**, or **On non-Retina displays**: draws text a little heavier. |
| **Color blending** (Mac) <Badge text="Next release" /> | **Native** | **Native** or **Linear**. Linear applies only when drawing with the GPU, and not over a wallpaper. |
| **Kitty keyboard protocol** <Badge text="Next release" /> | On | Programs that ask for it get every key with its modifiers. Applies to new tabs. |
| **Images** <Badge text="Next release" /> | On | Programs can draw pictures with kitty graphics. On Linux, sixel too where the system's VTE has it, and pictures more than 200 lines up in the scrollback aren't drawn. Applies to new tabs. |

Changes reach open tabs at once, except where the table says new tabs. On Linux the same settings
are also kept in `config.toml` under `[terminal]` (`kitty_keyboard`, `images`).

### Keys and pictures <Badge text="Next release" />

Keys with no text, such as <kbd>Home</kbd>, <kbd>F5</kbd> or the arrows, reach programs the way
xterm sends them. A program that asks for the kitty keyboard protocol gets every key with its
modifiers instead, while **Kitty keyboard protocol** is on.

On Linux, dropping files on a tab types their paths, quoted for the shell. A dropped image from
another app is saved first and its path typed; a dropped link is typed as text.

The terminal sets `TERM=xterm-256color`, `COLORTERM=truecolor` and `TERM_PROGRAM=AIDE`, and
otherwise passes on the environment AIDE was started with. A program in the terminal can copy to
your clipboard but can't read it.

### comux in the terminal <Badge text="Next release" />

On the Mac and on Linux, a tab running comux shows comux's sessions, spaces and agents in AIDE's own
controls around the terminal, instead of comux drawing them as text:

- A sidebar on the left: **Spaces** and **Agents**, each agent with its status (**working**,
  **ready**, **blocked**, **idle** or **done**). Click a row to go there; rest the pointer on one to
  preview its pane. Drag its edge to change its width; AIDE remembers it.
- A top bar: where you are, the **Work graph** buttons and the bell. The bell shows how many
  notifications are unread. Click it for **Notifications**: click one to go to its pane, **Dismiss**
  one, or **Clear all**.
- A status bar at the bottom: the session, its tabs, counters (**Bells**, **Waiting for you**,
  **Agents running**, **Unread notifications**), usage gauges and the clock.
- comux's pickers, prompts and confirms as a card over the tab. comux's own menus are a card too on
  Linux; on the Mac they open as a regular menu at the pointer. Right-click a row or a chip for its
  menu, such as **Jump to pane**, **Rename tab…** or **Rename session…**.

**Work graph** opens a graph of every agent's work beside the terminal; **Every agent's work, across
sessions** widens it to all sessions. The buttons never take the keyboard, so what you type keeps
going to the terminal.

To use a comux on a server, right-click a tab and choose **Open remote comux…**. Enter a host from
`~/.ssh/config` or `user@host`, and the comux command there if it isn't `comux`. It opens in a new
tab over `ssh`, with that server's sessions in the sidebar.

Only the tab on screen (in the Terminal section or the right panel) gets AIDE's controls. A tab you
can't see, or one without comux, shows comux as comux draws it itself. In a tab split into panes,
each pane runs its own comux, and the focused pane's comux gets the controls.

<Aside type="note">
The Terminal is part of the Mac app on the release page. A build for the Mac App Store, where the
sandbox doesn't allow starting a shell, would leave it out.
</Aside>

## SSH on the iPhone

<Aside type="caution">
The iPhone app is in a TestFlight beta and isn't public yet.
</Aside>

SSH is a built-in feature you turn on in **Settings › Core plugins › SSH** ("Saved hosts,
keys and passwords, terminals to your servers."). It adds an **SSH** section: open sessions on top,
then your saved hosts by group, with a search field.

On an iPad at full width, **SSH** is on the ribbon, where the Mac has the
terminal.

### Add a host

Choose **+** (**Add host**) and fill in:

- **Label**: what the list shows, such as "My server".
- **Host** (`example.com` or `192.168.0.2`), **Port** (22 by default) and **User**.
- **Group**, to sort hosts in the list (optional).
- **Sign in with**: **A password, asked each time**, or a key or saved password from your keychain.
  **Manage keychain…** opens the keychain.
- **Startup command**, such as `tmux attach`: offered in the session's menu as **Run startup
  command**, never run on its own.

Tap a host to connect. The session has its own terminal with the keyboard's accessory row, and a
menu with **Run startup command**, **Disconnect** and **Reconnect**. Sessions stay open while you
move around the app, and switching between notes on the device and a server doesn't close them;
turning SSH off does.

### comux on a host <Badge text="Next release" />

If a host runs comux, its row in the list has a **comux** button beside it. Tapping the row still
opens a shell; **comux** opens the host's comux instead, over the same sign-in, known hosts and
keychain. Touching and holding the row offers both: **Open a shell** and **Open comux**. An open
comux is listed with the sessions as "comux on" the host; swipe it to **Close** it.

The comux screen shows the panes as comux draws them, with:

- comux's tabs across the top, and a button on the left that opens **Spaces and agents** as a
  drawer.
- **Notifications** on the right, with the unread count, as a drawer from the right.
- comux's status line at the bottom, and its pickers, prompts, confirms and menus over the screen.
- A key bar over the keyboard: **esc**, **tab**, **ctrl**, the arrows, comux's keys (**^b**,
  **^f**, **^b a**, **^b c**, **^b n**, **^b p**, **^b z**), **^c** and **Hide**.
- The **Session** menu: **Work graph** (a graph of every agent's work, in a sheet), **New tab**,
  **New session…**, **Resume a conversation…**, **Reconnect** and **Disconnect**.

In the host's editor, the **comux** section has **comux command**: what runs on the host for comux.
Left empty, AIDE runs `comux proxy --stdio` from your login shell.

For a host that signs in with **A password, asked each time**, the comux screen asks for the
password. If the server turns it down, it says so and asks again.

### Agent notifications <Badge text="Next release" />

**Agent notifications**, in the host editor's **comux** section, is on by default. The next time you
open that host's comux, AIDE asks for permission to notify you and gives comux a key made for this
iPhone. comux then sends its agents' notifications through a push relay to Apple's push service,
sealed with that key: only this iPhone can read the title and text. The relay and Apple carry only
a placeholder. If comux turns notifications down, or is too old for them, the section says so.

Tapping a notification opens the SSH section on that host's comux, at the agent's pane. A
notification from the comux you're looking at doesn't show a banner. If its key is gone, the
notification reads "Agent notification" and "Open AIDE to see it." If SSH is turned off, tapping it
tells you to turn SSH on first.

Turning the switch off, or deleting the host, deletes its key at once. comux is told to forget this
iPhone the next time you connect to it.

### Keys and passwords

**Keychain** (in the section's menu) holds what you sign in with. Choose **Add**, then:

- **Password…**: a password saved under a name.
- **New ED25519 key…**: AIDE makes a key; its private half never leaves the iPhone's keychain. Open
  the key to **Copy public key** or **Share public key**, and add that line to
  `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` on the server.
- **Import a key…**: an ED25519 private key in OpenSSH format (`BEGIN OPENSSH PRIVATE KEY`),
  unencrypted or with a passphrase.
- **New Secure Enclave key…** <Badge text="Next release" />: where the iPhone or iPad has a Secure
  Enclave, it makes a P-256 key there. Its private half can't be read out, not even by AIDE, and it
  works only on that device. Copy or share its public key the same way.

Keys and passwords are kept in the iPhone's keychain, never in the host list.

### Known hosts

The first time you connect to a host, AIDE shows its key type and fingerprint. Check it with the
server's owner (`ssh-keygen -lf` on the server's host key) before **Trust and connect**. If a
host's key later changes, AIDE stops and shows both: someone may be intercepting the connection,
so choose **Replace the key and connect** only if you know the server was set up again.

**Known hosts** (in the section's menu) lists every key you've trusted; **Forget** removes one.
