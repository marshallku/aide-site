The morning briefing gathers what your day looks like and has your AI write it up as one short page, waiting for you when you wake up.

<PluginFacts id="briefing" />

## What it does

At 07:00 (or the time you set) the plugin:

1. Creates today's daily note if it doesn't exist yet, so the plugins that fill a new daily, such as [Todo carry](/docs/plugins/todo-carry/) and [Google Calendar](/docs/plugins/gcal/)'s agenda, have run by the time you look.
2. Gathers the day's material:
    - **Weather**: <Badge text="Next release" /> the next three days' forecast from Open-Meteo for the **Weather location** you set, anywhere, without an API key. A Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA) neighborhood or a forecast URL set in `plugins.json` still works instead (see [Local keys](#local-keys)).
    - **Schedule**: today's events and the next two days', from your Google calendars.
    - **Open todos**: your open top-level todos, for the AI to pick from.
    - **Yesterday**: yesterday's daily note.
    - **Health**: the three newest files in a folder of health exports, if you name one.
    - **Work items**: <Badge text="Next release" /> the GitHub, Linear and Jira sections of the `work/now` note, where [GitHub](/docs/plugins/github/), [Linear](/docs/plugins/linear/) and [Jira](/docs/plugins/jira/) list your open work, given to the AI as data from your tools, never as instructions. **Work items** (below) turns it off.
3. Asks your AI to write the briefing from that material only: the weather (morning and afternoon, low and high, chance of rain, what to wear), today's schedule in detail with a preview of the next two days, up to three todos that are realistic today (each as "[note] todo", offered as suggestions, never as pressure), yesterday's steps, sleep and exercise with the three-day trend, what stood out yesterday and what's unresolved, and one gentle line for the day. Anything without material is left out.
4. Writes it under `## ☀️ Morning briefing` in its own section of the day note, `briefings/{date}` (for example `briefings/2026-10-22`, titled "2026-10-22 (Thu) briefing"). Running again the same day replaces that section, and only that section.
5. Posts "☀️ Today's briefing" to the Feed, with the briefing as its text, and notifies you.

Material that can't be read is skipped: without a weather location there's no weather paragraph; without a Google sign-in there's no schedule. If the AI call fails, the plugin tries again every 10 minutes until it succeeds or the next morning comes. A morning missed while the computer was off or asleep is made up if it wakes within 3 hours.

## Before you start

- A [desktop host](/docs/self-hosting/desktop-host/) or your own server.
- An AI in **Settings › AI**: **Anthropic API key** or **My gateway**. The briefing has no AI-free form.
- Optional, for the schedule: <Badge text="Next release" /> the [Google Calendar](/docs/plugins/gcal/#set-it-up) plugin signed in to Google. The briefing reads the calendar through that plugin's sign-in, and never refreshes or saves its token. Setups with their own Google files (the `credentials` and `tokenFile` keys below, or the files Google Calendar read before this release) keep working.
- Optional, for the weather: the place's latitude and longitude. <Badge text="Next release" /> Open-Meteo covers anywhere; before this release, the weather needed a KMA neighborhood (Korea only) or a forecast URL in `plugins.json`.

## Set it up

<Steps>

1. Turn the plugin on: **Morning briefing** under **Settings › Core plugins**, then **Enabled** (on Linux, the switch on its row on the **Core plugins** page of Settings). See [Turn a plugin on](/docs/plugins/overview/#turn-a-plugin-on).
2. <Badge text="Next release" /> For the weather, set **Weather location** to your place's latitude and longitude, for example `51.51, -0.13`.
3. Set **Time** if 07:00 doesn't suit you.
4. If you want health data, add `healthDir` to `plugins.json` in your host's data folder ([where that is](/docs/plugins/overview/#local-settings)). See [Local keys](#local-keys).
5. To read the briefing from your daily note, link the day note from your daily template, or set **Write into** to `daily`.

</Steps>

## Settings

<PluginSettings id="briefing" defaults={{ at: "07:00", push: "Always (force)", note: "briefings/{date}", location: "" }} />

**Notification.** Until you choose, the briefing notifies you every morning, as `force` does. <Badge text="Next release" /> The choices read **Automatic**, **Always** and **Never**, and the picker shows the default, **Always**. Choose **Never** (`never`) to read it in the Feed only. **Automatic** lets the Feed's sorting decide whether it's worth a notification.

**Weather location** <Badge text="Next release" /> is `latitude, longitude` in decimal degrees, for example `51.51, -0.13` or `37.57, 126.98`. Anything else leaves the weather out, and the host log says `weather location isn't 'lat, lon'`. A KMA `weather` key in `plugins.json` wins over it.

**Work items** <Badge text="Next release" /> (on by default) adds the GitHub, Linear and Jira sections of the `work/now` note to the material, cut to 6 KiB. What you wrote in the note around them isn't included. The briefing runs on the server, so a device's **Never read** setting in **Settings › Assistant** doesn't apply to it: turn **Work items** off to keep `work/now` out of the briefing.

**Write into.** `daily` puts the briefing into today's daily note, in its own section at the end (or where your template puts `{{slot briefing.morning}}`). Any other value is a note path, with `{date}` replaced by the day in `YYYY-MM-DD`.

**Prompt.** Your own instructions replace the built-in ones entirely. The plugin still adds the date and all the gathered material after them, so write what you want done with it, for example "Three bullet points: weather, the first meeting, one todo."

<Badge text="Next release" /> The field comes filled in with the built-in prompt, in the language plugins write in, so you change it rather than start from nothing. The plugin keeps its answer rules after your text (Markdown for its own section, only the material given), whatever the prompt says. **Reset to default**, or clearing the field, brings back the built-in prompt. See [Prompts](/docs/plugins/overview/#prompts).

### Local keys

Under `"briefing"` in `plugins.json`:

| Key           | What it does                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `weather`     | The KMA forecast location: `code`, the ten-digit administrative code of your neighborhood (동), and `lat` and `lon`, its coordinates. Korea only. When set, it's used instead of **Weather location**. |
| `weatherUrl`  | Without `weather` or **Weather location**: any URL that returns a forecast as text or JSON. The plugin hands the first 6 KiB of the answer to the AI as it is. |
| `healthDir`   | A folder of health exports (steps, sleep, workouts), one file per day with the date in its name. The three newest by name are used. |
| `credentials` | Your own Google OAuth client file, for `tokenFile`. Empty: as Google Calendar does (see [its own OAuth files](/docs/plugins/gcal/#your-own-oauth-files)). |
| `tokenFile`   | Your own Google token file. Empty: Google Calendar's sign-in from the app, else `~/.config/life-assistant/google-token.json` when it exists. |

```json
{
    "timezone": "Asia/Seoul",
    "plugins": {
        "briefing": {
            "weather": { "code": "1111056000", "lat": "37.613", "lon": "126.9623" },
            "healthDir": "~/Health/daily"
        }
    }
}
```

The location above is a neighborhood of Jongno-gu, Seoul. The KMA forecast is read without an API key.

The briefing doesn't follow a `tokenFile` set for Google Calendar: with your own token file, name it here too.

<Aside>
The schedule comes from the calendars ticked in Google Calendar's own list, not from the **Show** switches in AIDE's **Calendars…** sheet.
</Aside>

## Troubleshooting

### The briefing didn't arrive

- Check that the plugin is on and that **Settings › AI** isn't **Rules (no AI)**.
- The host must be running at the briefing time, or wake within 3 hours of it. A Mac that stays asleep all morning skips that day.
- Look in the host log (its path is under **Log** in Settings › Host) for `daily task failed`, followed by the reason.

### The weather paragraph is missing or says the forecast failed

Without **Weather location**, `weather` or `weatherUrl` there's no weather. If the forecast didn't answer, or **Weather location** isn't `latitude, longitude`, the AI is told the lookup failed and leaves the weather out. Check the location (or, for KMA, `code`, `lat` and `lon`).

### There's no schedule

The plugin couldn't read the calendar; the host log says `calendar unavailable; briefing without it` with the reason. <Badge text="Next release" /> Sign in under [Google Calendar](/docs/plugins/gcal/#set-it-up)'s settings. The briefing looks it up at each briefing, so a sign-in counts from the next one.

### It's in the wrong language

Plugins write in the language of **Settings › Language** (Korean, or English for anything else). The briefing written today keeps its language; tomorrow's follows the change.

## Privacy

The weather request goes from the machine that runs the plugins to Open-Meteo (or the KMA website, or your `weatherUrl`), carrying only the location. Google is read with read-only calendar access. The gathered material (forecast, three days of events, your open todos, yesterday's daily note, your three newest health files) goes to the AI you set in **Settings › AI**, cut to 6 KiB per part. The briefing is stored as a note on your host or server.
