The Gmail plugin reads your mail so you don't have to check it all day. At 18:00 it writes what needs
a reply and what you're waiting on into your day note, and it pushes the mail your AI finds urgent.
It can only read: it never sends, labels, archives or deletes anything.

<PluginFacts id="gmail" />

## What it does

**Every 5 minutes** it asks Gmail which threads changed. It reads only headers and keeps only thread
ids.

**At 18:00** it writes a **📬 Mail** section into the day note (`briefings/<date>` unless you
choose another note):

- **Needs a reply**: inbox mail from the last 7 days that asks something of you. The plugin takes the
  latest message of at most 30 threads, leaves out newsletters and Gmail's Promotions, Social,
  Updates and Forums categories, cuts each to 2 KB of text and gives them to your AI to sort. Each
  line links to the thread in Gmail. Without an AI (or when it fails), every candidate is listed
  under **Mail to check** (selected without AI) instead.
- **Waiting for a reply**: threads where you wrote last, more than 3 days ago (and at most 30), and
  someone besides you was on it. Notes to yourself are left out.
- A count: how many messages came today, and how many were newsletters and notifications.

<Badge text="Next release" /> Each mail your AI found asks for a reply is also proposed as a todo, "Reply to *sender*: *what is asked*", once per message. AIDE adds it and tells you, or asks first, as [Todos it finds](/docs/guides/assistant/#todos-it-finds) says. Mail listed without the AI proposes nothing.

The section is the plugin's own: it rewrites it each day and leaves the rest of the note alone.
Link the day note from your daily template to see it from your daily note, or set **Write into** to
`daily` to put the section in the daily note itself ([Daily notes and
templates](/docs/guides/periodic-notes/)).

**Urgent mail.** When the AI marks a message urgent, the plugin pushes it once to your devices, titled
`📬 <subject>`, with who sent it and why it's urgent, linking to the thread. Turn off **Push urgent
mail** to stop that.

**A broken token** is said once a day: a pushed **Reconnect Gmail** item. <Badge text="Next release" /> The plugin also sets the refused token aside and shows **Sign in with Google** again under its settings, unless the token is your own `tokenFile` (see [Reconnect Gmail](#reconnect-gmail)).

If the computer was asleep at 18:00, the day's section is written when it wakes, if that's within 6
hours.

## Before you start

- **A server or desktop host running the server plugins.** On a desktop host that's the computer AIDE
  hosts the vault on ([Host on your desktop](/docs/self-hosting/desktop-host/)).
- **A Google account.** <Badge text="Next release" /> You sign in from the plugin's settings in the
  app, with AIDE's own Google client. Until this release you had to create an OAuth client in Google
  Cloud yourself; that still works ([Your own OAuth client](#your-own-oauth-client)).
- **An AI** for sorting the mail ([AI and models](/docs/guides/ai/)). Without one, the plugin still
  lists the mail, unsorted.

## Set it up

<Steps>

1. In AIDE, open **Settings** and choose **Gmail** under **Core plugins** (on Linux, the **Gmail** row
   on the **Core plugins** page of Settings).
2. Turn on **Enabled**. The plugin starts within a minute.
3. <Badge text="Next release" /> The plugin's status, under its settings, reads "Sign in to read your
   mail (read-only)…" with a **Sign in with Google** button. Choose it, pick your Google account and
   allow AIDE to read your mail.
    - **On the computer that runs the plugins** (the desktop host's own app): the browser comes back
      to AIDE by itself and says "AIDE is signed in to Google. You can close this tab."
    - **From a phone or another computer**: the browser ends on a page that doesn't load, at an
      address starting with `http://127.0.0.1`. Copy that whole address, paste it into the plugin's
      **Sign-in link** setting and save. The plugin picks it up within a few seconds.

   The status then reads "Connected to Gmail". A sign-in link works once, for 10 minutes; after that
   the button opens a new one.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Google may warn that it hasn't verified AIDE">
Until Google finishes reviewing AIDE's sign-in, the consent page can say **Google hasn't verified this app**. To go on, choose **Advanced**, then **Go to AIDE (unsafe)**. AIDE asks only to read, and the token stays on the computer that runs the plugins. While the review is pending, Google also limits how many people can sign in with AIDE's client; if it turns you away, use [your own OAuth client](#your-own-oauth-client).
</Aside>

The token stays in the plugin's state folder on the computer that runs the plugins. The first
section arrives at 18:00. Until then the plugin follows your threads every 5 minutes.

### Sign in from a terminal

On a server with no app nearby, you can sign in with a command instead:
`aide-plugins google-auth gmail`, run on the computer that runs the plugins. It opens Google's
consent page in that computer's browser and waits up to 10 minutes. Sign in, allow read access to
your mail, and the token is saved to `~/.config/aide-plugins/gmail-token.json`, where the plugin
looks for it. <Badge text="Next release" /> The command uses AIDE's own Google client unless you name
yours with `-credentials <file>`.

`aide-plugins` comes with AIDE's desktop host:

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="macOS">

It's inside the app. With AIDE in `~/Applications` (where the install script puts it):

```sh
~/Applications/AIDE.app/Contents/MacOS/aide-plugins google-auth gmail
```

If you moved AIDE to `/Applications`, use `/Applications/AIDE.app/Contents/MacOS/aide-plugins`.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Linux">

It's in the host runtime folder, one per AIDE version:

```sh
ls ~/.local/lib/aide/
~/.local/lib/aide/host-0.4.18/aide-plugins google-auth gmail
```

Use the `host-<version>` folder of the AIDE you have installed.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

The command prints `Saved the token to … Turn the plugin on in AIDE.` when it's done. `-token <file>`
saves the token elsewhere; then name it as `tokenFile` in the host's local plugin settings (below).
While the plugin reads a token file, it doesn't offer **Sign in with Google**. <Badge text="Next release" /> The token file names the client that made it, so the plugin always refreshes it with that same client.

### Your own OAuth client

Before this release, the plugin needed an OAuth client of your own, and setups that have one keep
working. To make one: in the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/), create a
project, enable the **Gmail API**, set up the consent screen (user type **External**, then **Publish
app** so its status is **In production**; left in **Testing**, Google expires your sign-in every 7
days), create an **OAuth client ID** of type **Desktop app** and download its JSON. Google warns that
it hasn't verified a client like this; it's your own, so choose **Advanced** and continue.

Name the file in the host's local plugin settings ([Local settings](/docs/plugins/overview/#local-settings)),
under `"gmail"`:

| Key           | What it does                                                                                      |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `credentials` | Your own OAuth client file. **Sign in with Google** then uses it; for `aide-plugins google-auth`, pass it as `-credentials <file>`. |
| `tokenFile`   | A token file to read instead of signing in from the app.                                          |

## Settings

- **Write into** takes a note path with `{date}` (`briefings/{date}`, `mail/{date}`…) or `daily` for
  the daily note.
- **Waiting for an answer after (days)**: a thread you wrote last is listed once it has waited this
  long, until it is 30 days old.
- **Sign-in link** <Badge text="Next release" /> is only for signing in from a phone or another
  computer (see [Set it up](#set-it-up)).

## Troubleshooting

### Nothing arrived at 18:00

- Check the plugin is **Enabled**, and that the host was running at 18:00 or woke within 6 hours.
- <Badge text="Next release" /> Look at the plugin's status under its settings. "Sign in to read your
  mail…" means it's still waiting for you to sign in.

### That sign-in link is old or already used

The pasted address came from an earlier attempt, or was already used. Choose **Sign in with Google**
again and paste the new address.

### This build can't sign in to Google

This copy of AIDE has no Google client built in. Use [your own OAuth client](#your-own-oauth-client).

### Reconnect Gmail

The token stopped working: you removed AIDE's access, changed your Google password, or (with your
own client) the consent screen was in **Testing** and the 7 days ran out. <Badge text="Next release" />
The plugin sets the refused token aside and shows **Sign in with Google** under its settings again:
choose it. That holds for the app's sign-in and for the token `aide-plugins google-auth gmail` saved
at `~/.config/aide-plugins/gmail-token.json`, which is skipped until it changes and never deleted.
Only a token file you named with `tokenFile` isn't set aside; the notification then says to run
`aide-plugins google-auth gmail` again for it.

### google: no refresh token came back

Google gave out a token before and won't give a lasting one again. Remove AIDE's access at
[myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions), then sign in again.

### google: no answer from the browser

`aide-plugins google-auth` waited 10 minutes for the consent page. Run the command again. It must run
on the computer whose browser you sign in with: Google sends you back to an address on that
computer.

### Everything is under Mail to check

The AI wasn't available or its answer couldn't be used, so the plugin listed every candidate. Check
[AI and models](/docs/guides/ai/).

## Privacy

- The plugin asks Google only for `gmail.readonly`. It can't send or change mail.
- The token stays on the computer that runs the plugins, in the plugin's own state folder (or in your
  own token file, with refreshed tokens kept in the plugin's state).
- It stores thread ids, not mail. The mail itself is read from Gmail each time.
- For sorting, up to 30 messages a day (sender, subject and at most 2 KB of text each) go to the AI you
  set up. The mail is given to it as data, not as instructions.
- The **📬 Mail** section (subjects, senders, why each needs a reply) is a note, synced to your
  devices. Urgent mail's subject and reason go out as a push notification.
